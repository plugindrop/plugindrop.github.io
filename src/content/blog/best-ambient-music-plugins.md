## FabFilter Pro-R 2

<!-- role:pick -->

<!-- pick:FabFilter Pro-R 2 -->

FabFilter Pro-R 2 is a professional reverb plug-in built for mixing and mastering work rather than just adding ambience. It runs on both Windows and macOS, and is available in VST2, VST3, AU, and AAX formats, so it fits into most major production setups.

The plug-in's defining feature is its Decay Rate EQ, which controls how the reverb tail decays across different frequency bands independently. In practice, this means the low end of a reverb tail can be shortened to keep bass frequencies from building up and muddying a mix, while the high end can be left to ring out longer, or the reverse can be done, without one range affecting the other. This kind of independent per-band decay control is not something most hardware-style or standard algorithmic reverbs offer, which sets Pro-R 2 apart as a tool built specifically for shaping how reverb sits inside a finished mix rather than simply recreating a space.

Beyond the Decay Rate EQ, Pro-R 2 also supports immersive audio formats, including configurations up to 9.1.6 Dolby Atmos, making it suitable for larger-scale or spatial mixing work as well as conventional stereo projects.

This makes the plug-in a strong fit for producers who handle their own mixing and mastering on ambient or atmospheric material, as well as anyone who needs fine, surgical control over exactly how a reverb tail behaves once it's sitting in a finished track.

## Output Portal

<!-- role:pick -->

<!-- pick:Output Portal -->

Portal is a granular synthesis effect built for altering audio input in real time. Rather than simply processing a sound, it re-synthesizes it, breaking the signal into grains while keeping the result closely related to the original source. It works on nearly any audio feed—virtual instruments, drum tracks, vocals, or live guitar—turning ordinary recordings into evolving, musical textures.

Grain controls, modulation routing, and built-in effects let the engine reshape incoming audio into atmospheric, granular soundscapes. A Time Stretch function slows down or stretches audio, either locked to the host's master tempo or running free and unsynced, so pacing can shift without breaking the arrangement. A separate Pitch Shift control changes the pitch of the material independently of its length or timing, making it possible to alter tonal character without disturbing rhythm. A Humanize feature adds natural-feeling variation to the sound's envelope, reducing repetitiveness and giving the output a more organic, less mechanical movement.

Best for: producers working with field recordings or acoustic sources who want to turn them into synthesized, ambient-sounding textures.

## How to choose

<!-- role:choose -->

Choosing among these comes down to what a given ambient setup is actually missing, not which plugin ranks highest overall. Productions that feel static and lifeless are the clearest case for Valhalla Supermassive, since its massive, sustaining reverb algorithms are built to keep tails evolving over long stretches, and the plugin carries no cost to try. When a track calls for recognizable vintage hardware color rather than a clean modern space, the Eventide SP2016 fits that role, since its room, stereo room, and high-density plate algorithms recreate a hardware-era character that algorithmic reverbs tend not to reproduce. Producers who handle their own mixing and mastering are better served by FabFilter Pro-R2 than by any hardware-modeled reverb, because its decay rate EQ allows the reverb's decay to be shaped separately across frequency bands, which matters when a dense ambient mix needs low-frequency tails controlled without dulling the high end. Anyone assembling a synthesis toolkit from nothing can combine Vital for spectral, morphing wavetable textures, Dexed for crystalline FM tones carried over from classic digital synthesis, and Odin 2 for phase-modulation oscillators paired with built-in phaser and chorus movement, forming a complete synthesis setup at no cost. Finally, work built around field recordings or other acoustic sources points toward Output Portal as the effect worth adopting first, since its real-time granular processing reshapes recorded audio into evolving, synthesized-sounding textures with an expressive, playable quality that static or offline granular tools don't offer.
