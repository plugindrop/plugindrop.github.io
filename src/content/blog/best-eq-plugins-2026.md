- **Formats:** VST, VST3, AU, AAX

The honest limitation: four bands go quickly on a busy master, and mid/side is paywalled behind the GE edition.

**Best for:** Producers who want dynamic EQ for free — especially on vocals, buses, and mastering, where static cuts aren't enough.

**Q: What's the difference between a parametric EQ and a dynamic EQ?**

A: A parametric EQ applies a fixed boost or cut at a frequency — it's always on, always the same amount. A dynamic EQ only engages when the signal at that frequency crosses a threshold, behaving like a frequency-specific compressor. Dynamic EQ is more transparent on material with variable levels — vocals, acoustic instruments — because it only processes when there's actually a problem, and leaves the tone alone during quieter moments.

## FAQ

<!-- role:faq -->

Q: What's the difference between a parametric EQ and a dynamic EQ?
A: A parametric EQ applies a fixed boost or cut at a chosen frequency, and that adjustment stays constant regardless of what the signal is doing. A dynamic EQ only kicks in when the signal at that frequency crosses a set threshold, acting like a compressor that targets just one frequency range. Dynamic EQ tends to sound more natural on material with varying levels, such as vocals or acoustic instruments, because it only reacts when a problem actually appears and otherwise leaves the tone untouched.

Q: Is a plugin like FabFilter Pro-Q 4 worth paying for when free options like TDR Nova exist?
A: For most people mixing their own music, a free dynamic EQ handles the bulk of everyday tasks well. The advantages of a premium option such as Pro-Q 4 — more available bands, a smoother interface, a tighter spectrum display, quicker ways to grab and adjust a frequency visually, a switch between zero-latency and linear-phase modes, and wider format support — matter most for people doing professional mixing and mastering work where the plugin gets used constantly. For someone earning income from mixing, the cost is a minor expense. For a hobbyist producer, starting with the free tools is reasonable.

Q: Are free EQ plugins good enough for professional use?
A: For the core jobs most producers need from an EQ — removing problem frequencies, adding brightness, and shaping the basic tone of a track — free plugins such as TDR Nova and TDR VOS SlickEQ GE handle them at no cost. The main benefits of paid tools like Pro-Q 4 come down to workflow speed, such as visual frequency grabbing, per-band dynamic processing, and a linear-phase option, along with added integration features, rather than any fundamental difference in sound quality.

Q: Do I need a linear-phase EQ?
A: A linear-phase EQ keeps the relationships between frequencies intact as they pass through the phase spectrum, and this matters most when making broad tonal changes on a master bus or full mix. On individual tracks during mixing, a standard minimum-phase EQ is usually the better choice, since it introduces less delay and avoids the faint pre-ringing artifacts that linear-phase processing can create. Voxengo Marvel GEQ offers a free linear-phase option for situations where that trade-off matters.

Q: Do I need separate EQ plugins for mixing and for mastering?
A: Not necessarily. A plugin such as FabFilter Pro-Q 4 can cover both jobs cleanly, using a fast, low-delay mode for mixing sessions and switching to a linear-phase mode for mastering passes. A dedicated mastering suite, such as iZotope Ozone 12, becomes worth considering when someone wants EQ, limiting, and stereo imaging tools to work together as one coordinated system rather than as separate plugins stacked in a chain.

Q: Can these EQ plugins be used in any DAW?
A: Yes. All of the plugins discussed support the VST3 format on both Windows and macOS. Most also support the AU format used by Logic and GarageBand, and the AAX format used by Pro Tools. A couple of the free options, including MEqualizer and TDR Nova, also still offer older 32-bit VST builds for people running older DAW setups. It's worth checking a given plugin's supported formats beforehand if AAX compatibility is a requirement.

Q: What is the best EQ specifically for mastering work?
A: FabFilter Pro-Q 4 used in its linear-phase mode, and a dedicated suite like iZotope Ozone 12, are both reasonable choices for mastering. Pro-Q 4 offers precise, surgical control inside an interface many people already know well, while a suite like Ozone 12 is built as an integrated mastering chain from the ground up. Among free tools, Voxengo Marvel GEQ and the dynamic mode in TDR Nova are solid starting points for mastering-related work without any cost.
