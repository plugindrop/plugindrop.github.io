### TDR Nova — Free dynamic EQ that competes with plugins ten times its price

<div class="video-embed"><iframe loading="lazy" src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/1CHFv4mWQYM" title="TDR Nova — sound demo" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture" allowfullscreen></iframe></div>

- **Developer:** Tokyo Dawn Records
- **Price:** Free (GE edition: ~$50)
- **Platforms:** Windows, macOS
- **Formats:** VST, VST3, AU, AAX
- **Best use case:** Dynamic frequency control on vocals, buses, and mastering passes
- **Limitation:** Mid/side mode and extra bands are locked behind the paid GE upgrade

TDR Nova is a parallel dynamic equalizer — meaning each band can function as a standard static EQ, a dynamic EQ that responds to level, or a compressor. The free version gives you four fully parametric bands plus a high/low shelf, a wideband dynamics section, and a spectrum analyzer. It's not a "good free EQ" — it's a legitimately great EQ that happens to be free. KVR's community forum and Reddit's r/mixingmastering consistently rank it as the best free dynamic EQ available, with many professional engineers using it alongside or instead of paid alternatives on critical tasks. The GE (Gentleman's Edition) paid upgrade adds additional bands, mid/side mode, and a few workflow refinements, but the free version handles 90% of mixing tasks without compromise.

Since the base plugin costs nothing, there's no "when to buy" question for most users — install it today. The only purchase decision is whether the $50 GE upgrade is worth it, and that's really a question of whether you need mid/side processing on a dynamic EQ specifically. If you don't know you need that feature yet, you don't need it yet.

**Best for:** Producers who want dynamic EQ for free — especially on vocals and buses where static cuts aren't enough.

### Ignite Amps PTEq-X — Free Pultec-style passive EQ emulation

<div class="video-embed"><iframe loading="lazy" src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/OBA0Omugrfg" title="Ignite Amps PTEq-X — sound demo" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture" allowfullscreen></iframe></div>

- **Developer:** Ignite Amps
- **Price:** Free
- **Platforms:** Windows, macOS
- **Formats:** VST, VST3
- **Best use case:** Pultec-style low-end fattening on kicks and bass; high-frequency air on vocals
- **Limitation:** Broad-stroke passive design; not a surgical corrective EQ

Ignite Amps PTEq-X emulates classic passive Pultec-style EQ circuits, covering both the EQP-1A (low and high boost/cut) and MEQ-5 (midrange) designs in a single interface. The characteristic passive Pultec behavior — where simultaneously boosting and cutting the same low-frequency band creates a gentle resonant shelf rather than canceling out — is a documented phenomenon of the original hardware that Ignite Amps' developer notes confirm the plugin models. Gearspace discussions describe PTEq-X as one of the more accurate free Pultec emulations available, particularly useful for adding low-end weight to kick drums and bass lines using the passive-style boost behavior.

**Best for:** Producers who want Pultec-style low-end fattening and high-frequency air on vocals and full mixes without paying for a commercial hardware emulation.

## How to Choose

- **If you're starting out and own nothing**, install MEqualizer and TDR Nova today — they cost nothing and cover static and dynamic EQ without compromise. Spend zero dollars until you've outgrown them.
- **If you need dynamic EQ on vocals or buses**, TDR Nova's free version handles this better than most producers expect. Only upgrade if you need mid/side mode, which requires the paid GE edition.
- **If you want analog character on individual tracks for free**, TDR VOS SlickEQ GE handles high-pass and mid-range shaping with vintage-leaning models, and Ignite Amps PTEq-X delivers passive Pultec-style low-end weight — both at zero cost.
- **If you're doing bus work or mastering and care about phase**, Voxengo Marvel GEQ's linear phase graphic EQ is the specific tool for this job — and it's free.
- **If you want one EQ to rule every session** — channel strips, surgical edits, mastering, dynamic EQ, M/S — FabFilter Pro-Q 4 at $179 is the correct answer. It's the industry reference for a reason.
- **If you're building a mastering chain from scratch and want an integrated solution**, iZotope Ozone 12 provides EQ, limiting, and metering in one coordinated environment — but if budget is the constraint, FabFilter Pro-Q 4 in linear phase mode on a mastering bus is a documented and effective alternative at lower total cost.
- **If your DAW's stock EQ frustrates you but you're not ready for Pro-Q 4**, the Waves SSL E-Channel on a Waves sale is the most cost-effective analog flavor upgrade, often available under $30.
- **If you produce at volume and want AI to speed up your decisions**, iZotope Neutron 4's Track Assistant and Masking Meter are genuinely useful — not gimmicks — and they cut the time you spend hunting clashing frequencies.

## FAQ
**Q: What's the difference between a parametric EQ and a dynamic EQ?**
A: A parametric EQ applies a fixed boost or cut at a frequency — it's always on, always the same amount. A dynamic EQ only engages when the signal hits a threshold, similar to a frequency-specific compressor. Dynamic EQ is more transparent on material with variable levels, like vocals or acoustic instruments, because it only processes when there's actually a problem.

**Q: Is FabFilter Pro-Q 4 really worth $179 when TDR Nova is free?**
A: For most producers just mixing their own music, TDR Nova handles the majority of tasks competently. **Q: Do I need a linear phase EQ?**
A: Linear phase EQ preserves the phase relationships between frequencies, which matters most on the master bus or stereo bus when you're making broad tonal adjustments. On individual tracks at mix stage, regular minimum phase EQs are usually preferable because they have lower latency and avoid pre-ringing artifacts.
