Vital — The Free Wavetable Synth That Changed Everything

- **Developer:** Matt Tytel

Vital is a spectral warping wavetable synthesizer with a feature set that matches paid options costing three times as much. ## Dexed

<!-- role:pick -->

<!-- pick:Dexed -->

Dexed — Free FM Synthesis and Full DX7 Compatibility

- **Developer:** Digital Suburban

- **Platforms:** Windows, macOS, Linux

- **Formats:** VST3, AU, AAX, LV2, Standalone

Dexed is a faithful emulation of the Yamaha DX7 — the six-operator FM synthesizer behind the electric pianos, metallic basses, and bell tones that defined 80s and 90s records. It reads original DX7 SysEx patch files, so thousands of real hardware presets spanning four decades of programming load straight in, and the six-operator engine is complete rather than a simplified subset. Every wavetable and analog synth above generates sound a different way; Dexed is the one free instrument on this list that covers FM territory properly.

FM programming has a deserved reputation for being unintuitive, and Dexed doesn’t hide that — building a patch from scratch means thinking in operators, algorithms, and modulation ratios, a steeper conceptual jump than turning a filter cutoff. The payoff is that full SysEx compatibility means you rarely have to start from a blank patch.

**Best for:** FM synthesis, DX7 patch compatibility, electric piano tones, and metallic or bell textures in lo-fi, funk, and future bass.

## u-he Hive 2

<!-- role:pick -->

<!-- pick:u-he Hive 2 -->

Hive 2 is a synthesizer from u-he, built as a lighter, faster alternative to the company's more demanding instruments. It runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux, and is available in VST, VST3, AU, and AAX formats.

The instrument blends virtual analog and wavetable oscillators with the same filter character found across u-he's line, but wraps them in an interface designed for quick, intuitive sound design rather than deep, time-consuming exploration. Patches that would take several minutes to build in a more complex synth can be put together in seconds here, thanks to low CPU demands and three distinct synth engines working together.

Sound shaping tools include drag-and-drop modulation assignment, so modulation sources can be linked to targets directly rather than through menus, along with four control pads that users can define and customize themselves for hands-on tweaking. A scale quantizer with a built-in list of predefined scales helps keep melodic and modulation patterns musically in tune.

The package also comes with an included Wavetables content set, along with supporting text files that document scripts written in a proprietary wavetable generation and editing language, plus a reference document covering that scripting language for anyone who wants to build their own wavetables.

Best suited for working producers who want genuine analog-style character and u-he's filter quality, without a steep learning curve or heavy strain on their system's processing power.

## u-he Diva

<!-- role:pick -->

<!-- pick:u-he Diva -->

Diva is u-he's flagship analog circuit simulation synthesizer, built to model the specific component behavior of classic instruments such as the Minimoog, Juno-60, and Jupiter-8. It aims for a level of circuit-level accuracy that has made it a reference point for other analog modeling plugins, since it simulates how the original circuits actually behave rather than just approximating their output sound.

The plugin runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux, and is available in VST, VST3, AU, and AAX formats. On the Mac side it supports OS X 10.9 or newer, including both Intel and Apple M1 machines, in 64-bit only. On Windows it runs on Windows 7 or newer in either 32-bit or 64-bit form. It needs at least 1GB of RAM, though more is recommended for smoother performance, and it benefits from a reasonably modern processor such as an Intel Nehalem-class chip or an Apple M1.

Compared with the original version, this edition cuts CPU usage by up to 40 percent and adds multithreading support so modern multi-core processors can handle more simultaneous voices without overloading. The preset library has grown substantially and leans toward rich, organic-sounding patches. The editor window can now be resized, and additional visual skins are planned for release soon after launch.

The overall selling point is authentic analog warmth and character that holds up against the vintage hardware it draws from, delivered at a small fraction of what it would cost to own the original instruments it emulates. It suits producers and composers who want genuine analog circuit character for pads, basses, and leads in any style that rewards warmth and harmonic depth.

## How to choose

<!-- role:choose -->

For buyers weighing this category of instrument, reasonable alternatives to look at include the Analog Monosynth Collection, the Analog Polysynth Collection, and Arturia Analog Lab Intro. These cover similar ground to one another, so choosing between them comes down to which specific sound engines and preset libraries actually fit the way someone wants to work.

Hive 2 combines three synthesis engines — including wavetable oscillators, standard waveforms, and tunable sub-oscillators — with a design built around low CPU usage and quick patching. Modulation is assigned by dragging a source onto a target, and four user-definable XY pads give hands-on control over several parameters at once. An eight-step shape sequencer drives four independent outputs, a separate arpeggiator and step sequencer support real-time recording, and a scale quantizer keeps patterns locked to a chosen scale from a pre-defined list. Seven effects, including distortion, chorus, and reverb, can be freely rearranged and applied in real time to shape the tone further. The package also comes with a set of wavetables, along with scripts written in a dedicated wavetable generation and manipulation language, plus documentation for anyone who wants to build their own wavetables. This is a synth to choose when speed and directness matter more than deep menu-diving, while still wanting real sound-design depth through its sequencing and modulation tools.

Diva is built around recreating authentic analog circuitry, modeling how classic vintage synthesizers actually behaved rather than approximating their sound from the outside. Q: What's the best free synth VST for FM synthesis?
A: Dexed. Q: Is Serum still worth buying in 2026 with so many good free synths?
A: Yes, for producers who work on electronic music regularly. Vital matches Serum in raw sound quality by most comparisons, but Serum's long-running preset ecosystem, large volume of available tutorials, and refined workflow still make it the faster tool for professional output. Q: What's the best synth VST for EDM and dance music production?
