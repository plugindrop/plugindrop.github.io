## Soundtoys Decapitator
<!-- role:pick -->
<!-- pick:Soundtoys Decapitator -->

Soundtoys Decapitator suits mixers and producers who want to push vocals, drums, or synths beyond clean processing, giving tracks character, warmth, or edge rather than a too-polite sound. Users can dial in one of its five analog saturation models, shape the result with the tone control, engage Punish for extreme drive, and blend in dry signal via the mix knob for parallel processing without separate routing.

## Soundtoys PanMan
<!-- role:pick -->
<!-- pick:Soundtoys PanMan -->

Soundtoys PanMan suits producers and mix engineers who want to move beyond basic left-right panning into rhythmic, tempo-locked stereo effects, including those who want their track's own dynamics (like a snare hit or vocal loudness) to drive the motion. They can trigger panning manually, via host automation, or by audio threshold, then shape it using Step or Shape rhythm modes, the vintage-inspired trigger divider, and any of the seven analog emulation modes for added tone.

## Soundtoys PrimalTap
<!-- role:pick -->
<!-- pick:Soundtoys PrimalTap -->

PrimalTap suits producers and sound designers chasing vintage digital-delay character—especially guitarists wanting shimmering 80s echoes or anyone exploring lush chorus, tape-like flanging, and sci-fi warble textures. Users can shape its sound with the Multiply knob for octave-jump and lo-fi pitch effects, sweep the VCO section via the Rate and Adjust controls for smooth or glitchy modulation, and use the Freeze control to sample, loop, and warp incoming audio.

## Soundtoys Devil-Loc Deluxe
<!-- role:pick -->
<!-- pick:Soundtoys Devil-Loc Deluxe -->

Devil-Loc Deluxe suits producers and mixers who want an extreme, gritty limiting effect for making drums and room mics sound huge, or for adding dark, driven character to lo-fi loops, vocals, and acoustic guitar. Use the Darkness control to roll off crunchy highs for a warmer tone, switch between fast and slow release times for different textures, and dial the Mix knob to blend the crushed signal back with the dry source so the effect can be automated without losing the track's core.

## Soundtoys MicroShift
<!-- role:pick -->
<!-- pick:Soundtoys MicroShift -->

Soundtoys MicroShift suits mixers looking to widen background vocals, lead guitars, or synths without a complicated setup, offering three hardware-inspired flavours of stereo widening plus a simpler Little MicroShift option for quick, fuss-free spreading. Users can dial in the character of that width with the Delay, Detune, and Focus controls, adjusting pitch-shift amount, delay time variation, and frequency range to keep the effect clean rather than muddy.

## Soundtoys Tremolator
<!-- role:pick -->
<!-- pick:Soundtoys Tremolator -->

Soundtoys Tremolator suits guitarists and keyboard players chasing the classic pulsing tremolo of amps like the Fender Vibrolux or a Wurlitzer electric piano, as well as remixers who want a rhythmic effect rather than a smooth wobble. They can dial in a vintage-style tremolo with the custom LFO waveshapes, switch to a squarewave and use the programmable Rhythm Editor with MIDI sync to turn it into a tempo-locked auto-gate, and apply the Dynamics controls to let playing volume shape the modulation's speed and depth in real time.
