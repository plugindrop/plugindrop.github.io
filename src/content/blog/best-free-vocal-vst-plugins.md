## TDR Nova

<!-- pick:TDR Nova -->

TDR Nova — Free Dynamic EQ That Handles De-essing and Beyond

- **Developer:** Tokyo Dawn Records

- **Platforms:** Windows, macOS

- **Formats:** VST, VST3, AU, AAX

TDR Nova is a parallel dynamic EQ where each band can respond dynamically to incoming signal level — meaning you can tame a harsh 3kHz spike only when it gets loud. On vocals, this is ideal for controlling presence peaks, harsh resonances, and sibilance without flat-lining the frequency response. CPU usage is modest and the sound quality competes directly with paid processors well above its price point, which is why it stays on nearly every "must-have free plugin" list in 2026.

**Best for:** Dynamic vocal EQ, frequency-specific compression, and transparent de-essing.

**Skip it if:** you hunt resonances by eye — the free Nova has four bands and no spectrum analyzer, so surgical work is slower than on an FFT EQ (the paid GE adds both).

Wider (Polyverse) — Mono-Safe Stereo Widening

- **Developer:** Polyverse Music

- **Platforms:** Windows, macOS

- **Formats:** VST, VST3, AU, AAX

Wider uses mid-side processing to expand the stereo field without introducing phase cancellation in mono — a critical distinction when your track might be played on a phone speaker or a club system. **Best for:** Adding controlled stereo width to vocal channels and backing vocal buses without mono compatibility issues.

**Skip it if:** you want per-band width control or metering — Wider is one knob, and pushed hard on a mono lead it hollows out the center. Keep it gentle; save heavy widening for stereo stacks.

## How to choose

<!-- role:choose -->

Producers choosing among these should match the plugin to what the vocal actually needs. Anyone wanting one plugin to handle the whole chain should start with Analog Obsession CHANNELSTRIP, since it combines a high-pass filter, EQ, and compression in a single well-designed interface and covers most of what a typical vocal requires. Anyone prioritizing transparent pitch correction should begin with MAutoPitch, then step up to Waves Tune Real-Time for lower latency and formant control, or to Auto-Tune Pro for professional Graph Mode editing when the session calls for it.

Producers working in pop, hip-hop, or electronic music who want the exaggerated, robotic "effect" autotune sound get more creative flexibility from Graillon 2 than from any other free alternative, because of its dedicated pitch-lock effect. When a vocal sounds harsh or sibilant, TDR Nova works well as a dynamic de-esser that offers surgical control, while T-De-Esser Plus is the better choice for a dedicated, faster setup aimed specifically at sibilance.

Vocals that need more space and size benefit from pairing TAL-Reverb-4 on a send for a warm, vintage plate character with Valhalla Supermassive on a second send for larger, more ambient spaces; the two complement each other well within the same session. On a limited CPU or laptop, MAutoPitch, Wider, and the TDR plugins are the lightest options to run, while the more demanding Chow Tape Model is better saved for tracks where its tape character is worth the added processing load.

A simple vocal chain built from these tools can start with Analog Obsession CHANNELSTRIP handling the high-pass filter, EQ, and compression, followed by TDR Nova for dynamic de-essing, then Chow Tape Model for a touch of tape warmth, TAL-Reverb-4 placed on a reverb send, and Wider applied gently on the vocal bus. Put together in that order, this forms a complete, professional-grade vocal signal path built entirely from free tools. For producers who want a faster way to reach the same result, iZotope Nectar 4 consolidates this entire chain into a single plugin window.

## FAQ

<!-- role:faq -->

Q: What is the best free vocal VST plugin in 2026?
A: MAutoPitch is the best all-around free vocal plugin to install first. It delivers transparent, low-latency pitch correction that holds up well on finished tracks, works across every major DAW, and costs nothing to use. For a complete free chain, pair it with a combined EQ-and-compression channel strip plugin and a warm plate-style reverb plugin.

Q: Are free vocal VST plugins good enough for professional music production?
A: Yes. Plugins such as Valhalla Supermassive, Klanghelm MJUC jr, and MAutoPitch are used on commercial releases by working engineers. The quality gap between free and paid tools has narrowed a great deal in recent years, especially for reverb and compression. The main limitation tends to be workflow speed rather than sound quality.

Q: What is the difference between MAutoPitch and Graillon 2?
A: MAutoPitch is built for transparent pitch correction — fixing notes that are out of tune without the listener noticing anything was changed. Graillon 2 is a more creative vocal processor, offering pitch shifting, bit crushing, and ring modulation aimed at producing noticeable effects. Use MAutoPitch when correcting a performance, and use Graillon 2 when shaping character or doing sound design on a vocal.

Q: Do these free plugins work in every DAW?
A: The plugins covered in this guide support VST3 on Windows and VST3 or AU on macOS, which covers hosts such as Ableton Live, FL Studio, Logic Pro, Studio One, Reaper, Bitwig, Cubase, GarageBand, and most other current software. For older setups that only support VST2, or for AAX compatibility needed in Pro Tools, check each plugin's own product page.

Q: What order should I use vocal plugins in my signal chain?
A: A standard starting point runs: high-pass filter, then de-esser, then EQ, then compression, then saturation, then reverb or delay on a send, and finally stereo width. A combined channel-strip plugin can cover the high-pass-filter-through-compression stages in a single insert; place a dedicated de-esser or dynamic EQ before it on recordings with noticeable sibilance.

Q: What free plugin gives the T-Pain or hard autotune effect?
A: Two approaches work here. Setting MAutoPitch to maximum speed and depth, locked to the song's key, produces a clean hard-tune sound. Alternatively, Graillon 2's free version is built specifically for the robotic pitch-lock effect and adds bit crushing and ring modulation for a more aggressive, distinctive result.

Q: Is Valhalla Supermassive really free, and what is the catch?
A: It is genuinely free, with no time limit, no watermark, and no features held back. There is no catch in practical use. The one thing worth noting is where to get it: rather than being listed on general plugin marketplaces, it needs to be downloaded directly from the maker's own website.
