## Working With the Interface and Modes

Ping-pong modes send delay repeats between left and right, and Mid/side processing lets you treat the center and the sides of a signal independently. Live modulation visualization for targets and sources shows which parameters are linked and what is driving them, and Interactive delay and filter displays keep the tap positions and filter curves visible on screen as you edit. Between them, these controls cover both the layout choices for a delay pattern and the view you work from while shaping it. ## Building rhythmic delay patterns
<!-- role:workflow -->

A producer piecing together a beat might want echoes that land on different subdivisions instead of one repeating hit. With up to sixteen taps, each given its own time and level, you can spread a delay pattern across a bar rather than just bouncing a single echo back. Ping-pong modes move those taps between left and right, so the pattern reads as part of the rhythm instead of a static repeat. Feedback and cross-feedback, and modulatable freeze option, let you choose whether a tap fades out after a few repeats or holds in place once you want it locked in. Mid/side processing keeps the delayed taps from crowding the center of the mix. Interactive delay and filter displays show where each tap sits, which helps once several taps are running together. The result is a pattern built from the delay itself, not just an effect added on top.
