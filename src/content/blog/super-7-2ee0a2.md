## What you actually get

UVI lists Super 7 as inspired by the Roland MKS-7, blending analog 106 synth architecture with 707-style PCM drum sounds in one instrument. That combination means you get synth and drum engines built around the same hardware lineage, rather than bolting a drum machine onto an unrelated synth.

- The product listing notes patches are multi-sampled in three variations — clean, chorus, and noise — which gives each sound a bit of hardware-style character depending on which variation you load.
- According to the listing, drum sounds span 606, 626, 707, 808, and 909 machines plus custom Drum Designer kits, so classic and custom drum textures sit in the same browser.
- UVI lists 6 discrete layers with independent control over drums, bass, melody, and synth parts, meaning a single patch can carry a full arrangement sketch rather than one sound at a time.
- Each layer carries its own amp envelope, multimode filter, drive, phasor, EQ, delay, and reverb, according to the product page, so shaping an individual layer doesn't require leaving the instrument.
- The listing also notes an arpeggiator/phraser with editable parameters and a preset library, plus 260+ factory patches covering synthwave, electro, house, and modern analog-inspired genres.

## Where it fits in a session

This sits well at the sketch stage, when you want a cohesive synth-and-drum palette without patching together separate plugins. The six-layer structure makes it workable for building a verse or chorus idea with bass, melody, and drums loaded from one instrument window.

It also fits sound-design passes where the synthwave, electro, or house patch categories match the track's genre. Because drums and synth share one amp envelope, filter, and effects chain per layer, it's a reasonable spot for quick tonal matching between a kick and a bassline without routing between plugins.
