## VSCO2 Community Edition

<!-- role:pick -->

<!-- pick:VSCO2 Community Edition -->

VSCO2 Community Edition — Open-source orchestral foundation for the full ensemble

- **Developer:** Versilian Studios

- **Platforms:** Windows, macOS, Linux

- **Formats:** SFZ (compatible with Kontakt, sforzando, and SFZ-capable samplers)

The Versilian Studios Community Orchestra 2 (VSCO2 CE) is a free, open-source orchestral sample library covering strings, brass, woodwinds, and percussion. Sampling density doesn't match premium libraries, but it provides a complete orchestral palette at zero cost. It loads in any SFZ-capable sampler — the free sforzando player from Plogue is the most common pairing.

**Buy decision:** Free, no purchase decision required — the only cost is disk space and setup time. Good to have installed as a backup/fallback orchestral palette even if you own a premium library, since it's small enough not to bloat a sample drive.

**Best for:** Producers and composers who need a complete orchestral library for free, particularly for game audio or indie film.

## How to choose

<!-- role:choose -->

Someone with no budget at all should download BBCSO Discover first; its recording quality holds up for professional work, and the only real limitation is a smaller set of playable articulations, not sound quality. Producers working in pop, R&B, or soul styles are generally better served by Session Strings Pro 2, since it runs in the free Kontakt Player and its contemporary character fits modern mixes better than a traditional orchestral library would; it's worth waiting for a sale rather than paying full price right away. For anyone whose main priority is legato performance quality on a moderate budget, Cinematic Studio Strings is the strongest pick at that tier, assuming the full paid version of Kontakt is already owned; because it rarely goes on sale in any meaningful way, there's little reason to delay a purchase in hopes of a markdown. Composers building a full professional scoring template should look to Spitfire BBC Symphony Orchestra Core as the primary full-orchestra library, and it's also the best candidate on this list for timing a purchase around a seasonal sale period. Finally, anyone doing post-production, sync licensing, or stem-delivery work will benefit from EastWest Hollywood Strings' multi-mic recording setup, which gives the kind of flexibility professional pipelines increasingly expect; since it's offered as a subscription rather than a one-time purchase, it can be tried out during a discounted trial period before committing to it long-term.

## FAQ

<!-- role:faq -->

Q: Do I need Kontakt to use strings VST plugins?
A: No. BBC Symphony Orchestra, BBCSO Discover, and LABS Strings each run in their own free dedicated player. Berlin Strings runs in the free SINE player. Session Strings Pro 2 runs in the free version of Kontakt. Cinematic Studio Strings, Adagio Strings, and CineStrings CORE require the full paid version of Kontakt, so if you don't already own that license, its cost should be factored in when comparing the overall cost of those libraries against the others.

Q: What's the difference between BBCSO Discover and BBC Symphony Orchestra Core?
A: Both draw on the same orchestral recordings. Discover is free but limited to long notes and basic short notes. Core adds the full range of articulations, including spiccato, sul ponticello, tremolo, col legno, and other extended techniques, along with additional control over microphone positions. Core is the step up for professional scoring work, while Discover remains a legitimate tool to start with.

Q: Are these libraries CPU- and RAM-intensive?
A: Hollywood Strings is the most demanding option, due to its multiple simultaneous microphone positions. The free starter tier and the textural strings instrument are comparatively light on system resources. Berlin Strings and BBC Symphony Orchestra Core fall in between — demanding when used in a full orchestral template, but manageable on modern hardware with adequate memory and proper sample-streaming settings.

Q: Can these plugins run in any DAW?
A: All the libraries covered support VST3 and AU, which covers Ableton Live, Logic Pro, FL Studio, Cubase, Studio One, and Reaper. Pro Tools users need AAX, which every paid option here provides. VSCO2 CE requires a compatible SFZ host; a free SFZ-compatible player called sforzando is the standard pairing.

Q: Which strings library is best for film scoring specifically?
A: Three libraries stand out for strong legato performance and realism: BBC Symphony Orchestra Core, Cinematic Studio Strings, and Hollywood Strings. Choosing between them comes down to overall budget and whether microphone flexibility or legato scripting matters more for your workflow, along with whether your timeline allows you to wait before buying.

Q: Is it ever worth buying right away instead of waiting?
A: Yes, in two situations: when a library rarely changes price at all, or when a paid project with a deadline this month needs capabilities that your existing free tools can't cover. Outside of those two situations, checking current pricing before buying is generally worthwhile, since price changes for some of these products can be substantial.
