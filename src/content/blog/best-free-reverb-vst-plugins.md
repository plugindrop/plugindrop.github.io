## FAQ

**Q: What is the best free reverb VST plugin in 2026?**
A: Valhalla Supermassive. It is free, cross-platform, CPU-efficient, and used by professional producers across every genre. Start there.

**Q: Do free reverb VSTs actually sound good enough for professional releases?**
A: Yes, at the top of this list. Supermassive, TAL-Reverb-4, Convology XT, and OrilRiver compete with mid-tier paid plugins. The gap with top-tier commercial options like FabFilter Pro-R exists, but it is narrower than most producers expect until they hear them side by side.

**Q: Are these plugins compatible with my DAW?**
A: The cross-platform options — Supermassive, TAL-Reverb-4, Dragonfly, Protoverb — support VST3 and AU, covering Ableton, Logic, FL Studio, Cubase, Reaper, and Studio One. Pro Tools requires AAX format; check each plugin's format listing specifically before building sessions around it.

**Q: What is the difference between convolution and algorithmic reverb?**
A: Convolution reverb uses recordings of real physical spaces (impulse responses) — highly realistic but less flexible and more CPU-intensive. Algorithmic reverb generates space mathematically — more creative, more controllable, and lighter on CPU. Professional setups typically use both. Use Convology XT when you need a real space; use Supermassive when you need to design one.

**Q: Are older freeware plugins like OldSkoolVerb and Ambience still safe to use in 2026?**
A: Yes, but verify 32-bit compatibility first. Several older freeware plugins are 32-bit only, which requires a bridge tool like jBridge on modern 64-bit DAWs. The plugins themselves are safe — confirm your DAW handles 32-bit before committing them to a template or live session.
