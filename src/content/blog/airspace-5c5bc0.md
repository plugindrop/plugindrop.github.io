## What you actually get

Mode Audio lists Airspace as a combination of convolution reverb and stereo delay built for spatial processing in one plugin. The dual convolution engines let you blend two impulse responses at once, which means you can layer a room sound against a plate or spring character without stacking separate plugin instances.

According to the product listing, each impulse response's perceived "size" can be scaled from 20% to 500%, with full control over AHR amplitude enveloping, pre-delay, and gain. That range covers everything from a tightened-down closet to a cavern, without needing a different IR file for each size.

The product listing also notes dual 4-band EQ built in for shaping each impulse response independently, so you can carve low end out of one engine while brightening the other. On the preset side, the vendor lists 240+ presets split across six folders: Ambient, Blur, Cosmos, Reflect, Transform, and Warp, with the option to save your own.

## Where it fits in a session

Convolution reverb slots naturally on a send bus for drums, vocals, or pads where you want a believable space rather than an algorithmic wash. The dual-engine design makes Airspace useful for blending a realistic room with a more textured, processed IR on the same send, which suits ambient and cinematic sound design work in particular.

The built-in delay means you can use Airspace as a single insert for rhythmic, spatial tails on synths or guitars instead of chaining a separate delay plugin before the reverb. The six preset folders (Ambient, Blur, Cosmos, Reflect, Transform, Warp) point toward use cases ranging from subtle room ambience to heavily processed, abstract textures.
