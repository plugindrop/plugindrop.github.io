## What you actually get

FabFilter lists support for up to 24 EQ bands, which covers everything from a single surgical notch to a full multiband mastering chain on one instance. The product listing also notes universal filter slope support across all filter types, up to 96 dB/octave, so steep high-pass and low-pass cuts stay clean instead of ringing.

Auto Gain and Gain Scale are listed as well, which matter for level-matched A/B comparisons while you're dialing in a curve. According to the product listing, there's an optional piano-roll display that quantizes EQ frequencies to musical notes, useful when you're carving space around a specific pitch rather than eyeballing a frequency number.

A few more specifics from the listing worth knowing before you buy:

- Different display ranges (3 dB and 6 dB for mastering, 12 dB and 30 dB for mixing) so the graph scale matches the task at hand.
- An intelligent solo mode for isolating problem frequencies band by band.
- Phase Invert, plus a choice of zero latency, linear phase with adjustable latency, or FabFilter's Natural Phase mode.
- Extreme CPU optimization, which the listing says allows hundreds of instances in a session without heavy load.

## Where it fits in a session

Pro-Q 4 slots in almost anywhere EQ is needed: tracking cleanup on individual channels, surgical notching on busy mix buses, or tonal shaping on a master chain. The linear phase and Natural Phase options make it a reasonable pick for mastering, where phase coherence across the stereo field matters more than in a quick mix pass.

The piano-roll frequency display is specifically useful when EQ'ing tonal elements like bass or vocals, since you can match a boost or cut to the note being played instead of guessing a Hz value. The mixing-oriented 12 dB and 30 dB display ranges, versus the 3 dB and 6 dB mastering ranges, mean the same plugin adapts its visual scale depending on which stage of the session you're in.
