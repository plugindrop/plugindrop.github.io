## What you actually get

FabFilter lists support for up to 24 EQ bands, which is enough headroom for detailed surgical work on a single channel without stacking multiple EQ instances. Universal filter slopes up to 96 dB/oct are also listed, covering everything from gentle tonal shaping to steep brick-wall cuts on problem frequencies.

According to the product listing, Auto Gain and Gain Scale are built in, so level changes from aggressive boosts get compensated automatically during A/B comparisons. The listing also includes an optional piano roll display that quantizes EQ bands to musical notes, which is useful when you're tuning resonances to match a key or a specific pitched element.

FabFilter lists an intelligent solo mode for isolating problem frequencies on a band-by-band basis, and a Natural Phase mode alongside the standard zero-latency and linear-phase options. The listing also notes extreme CPU optimization, with low enough overhead to run many instances across a session.

## Where it fits in a session

Pro-Q 4 sits naturally on individual tracks during mixing, where the solo mode and steep slopes help isolate and remove unwanted resonances before any tonal shaping happens. The mastering-oriented display ranges (3 dB and 6 dB) make it suited to the final stereo bus, where moves are usually small and precise.

The linear-phase and Natural Phase modes matter most on parallel processing chains or anywhere phase coherence across multiple tracks is a concern, such as drum bus EQ. The piano-roll quantization is a fit for tonal mixing tasks like notching out a bass note's harmonic or matching an EQ boost to a vocal's key.
