## How to Choose

- **If you produce boom-bap, lo-fi, or neo-soul**, prioritize soul chop packs and vintage vocal texture packs. You'll spend 80% of your time in those two categories, and quality of performance matters more than quantity of samples.
- **If you produce trap, drill, or pluggnb**, look for hook packs that include ad lib sets and specify dry vs. wet recordings — processing a dry vocal is almost always faster than unwrapping a printed autotune chain.
- **If you produce commercial pop or EDM**, invest in packs with accurate BPM and key metadata. Time spent hunting for pitch matches is time not spent on arrangement. Key-labeled files are non-negotiable.
- **If you produce house or club music**, look for full acapella versions alongside loop sets. DJs and remixers you collaborate with will need isolated vocal stems — having them ready prevents back-and-forth.
- **If you're scoring for sync or film**, gospel choir stacks and neo-soul harmonies are the highest-value categories. Emotional vocal textures are what music supervisors reach for when a scene needs to land.

## FAQ

**Are vocal sample packs from Loopmasters truly royalty-free?**
Yes. Loopmasters packs are cleared for commercial use, including streaming releases, sync placements, and broadcast. Always verify licensing terms on the specific pack page before use in a sync context, as some packs have restrictions on re-selling the samples in competing sample libraries.

**Can I use vocal sample packs if I release music on Spotify or Apple Music?**
Generally yes — royalty-free means you don't owe ongoing royalties to the sample publisher. However, you may still face Content ID claims on streaming platforms if another producer used the same sample prominently. Building unique arrangements around shared samples is the practical mitigation.

**What's the difference between a vocal loop and a vocal one-shot?**
A loop is a rhythmically and harmonically complete phrase designed to repeat seamlessly — useful for verses, hooks, and pads. A one-shot is a single vocal event (a syllable, a word, a note) designed to be triggered individually in a sampler. Most professional packs include both.

**How do I pitch-match a vocal sample to my track's key?**
Every major DAW has built-in pitch-shifting tools — Ableton's Complex Pro, Logic's Flex Pitch, and FL Studio's Pitcher all handle vocal material well. For finer work, Melodyne is the industry standard. Always work from dry (unprocessed) samples when pitch-shifting — autotune artifacts compound under further pitch manipulation.

**What sample rate and bit depth should I look for?**
24-bit WAV at 44.1 kHz is the professional standard and what most Loopmasters packs deliver. 44.1 kHz is sufficient for all streaming and broadcast contexts. 48 kHz packs are worth prioritizing only if you're producing for video or film, where 48 kHz is the post-production standard.
