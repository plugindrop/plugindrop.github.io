## Sampler-Based Electric Pianos

Sampler-based electric pianos record a real physical instrument at multiple velocity layers and key positions, then play back those recordings in real time through a sample engine. The result is a high-fidelity snapshot of a specific instrument on a specific day — typically offering superior out-of-the-box realism compared to modeling, with less flexibility for real-time sound design.

## Physical Modeling Electric Pianos

Physical modeling synthesizes sound by simulating the full mechanical chain of the instrument — tines, tone bars, pickup position, tremolo circuit, amplifier — in real time. Every note responds uniquely to velocity, timing, and sustain, just as the real instrument does. The trade-offs are higher CPU demand and a steeper learning curve, but the upside is infinite parameter control and zero disk footprint from sample files.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between a Rhodes and a Wurlitzer?**
The Rhodes uses metal tines that vibrate against a tone bar, producing a bell-like sustain with a clean, slightly metallic shimmer. The Wurlitzer uses a vibrating metal reed near a pickup plate, creating a buzzier, more nasal tone that breaks up pleasingly when overdriven. Most physical modeling plugins like Lounge Lizard EP-4 include both architectures; sample libraries typically specialize in one.

**Do I need a full Kontakt license to use Scarbee Mark I?**
No. Scarbee Mark I runs in the free Kontakt Player, which is available as a no-cost download from Native Instruments. The instrument loads and plays without restriction inside the free Player with no time limits or unlock requirements.

**What is the CP-80 and why is it hard to find in VST form?**
The Yamaha CP-80 is an electric grand piano — a full-size grand with electromagnetic pickups rather than a traditional acoustic soundboard. It produces a brighter, harder attack than either a Rhodes or Wurlitzer and defined the sound of many 1980s records. Sampling it accurately requires capturing a larger instrument with more mechanical complexity, which is why credible CP-80 emulations are rare outside of premium libraries like Keyscape.

**Are these plugins reliable for live performance?**
Lounge Lizard EP-4 ships with a fully functional standalone application, making it stage-ready without a DAW. Scarbee Mark I requires Kontakt Player or a DAW host, but runs stably in Mainstage and Ableton Live for live use. Pianoteq 8 also includes a standalone mode and is particularly valued for live performance because of its low CPU demand.

**Which electric piano VST sounds most realistic out of the box?**
For Rhodes specifically, the consensus across professional recording forums points to Scarbee Mark I as the most immediately convincing sample library without any additional tweaking. For Wurlitzer, Lounge Lizard EP-4's modeling captures the reed buzz and distortion character in a way that sample-based approaches tend to flatten. For CP-80, Keyscape is the reference most engineers reach for.
