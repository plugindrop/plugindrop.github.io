## Wavesfactory Trackspacer

<!-- role:pick -->

<!-- pick:Wavesfactory Trackspacer -->

Character & Utility

Trackspacer works differently from a typical sidechain compressor. Instead of turning down the overall volume of a track whenever the sidechain source is active, it listens to the frequency content of that sidechain signal and carves out matching space in the main signal's spectrum, applying what amounts to an inverse EQ curve rather than a blanket volume cut. The result is a tool that behaves like a sidechain compressor in setup — a trigger signal shaping how another signal responds — but operates with far more nuance, since it only reduces the frequencies that are actually competing for space rather than ducking the whole track.

In practice this makes it suited to situations where two sounds occupy overlapping frequency ranges and need to coexist without one audibly pumping under the other. A bass part and a kick drum, a lead vocal and a rhythm guitar, or any pair of competing elements can be given room to breathe without the obvious volume dips that come from conventional sidechain compression. Because the effect targets specific bands rather than the whole signal, the processed track keeps more of its natural dynamics and tone intact while still making space for the triggering sound. This frequency-aware approach to mix separation is the plugin's defining trait, setting it apart from ordinary dynamics processors that only react to overall level.

## FabFilter Pro-Q 4

<!-- role:pick -->

<!-- pick:FabFilter Pro-Q 4 -->

FabFilter Pro-Q 4 is an equalizer plug-in that offers up to 24 bands for shaping sound. What sets it apart is Spectral dynamics, a feature built for treating problem frequencies with precision and efficiency.

This plug-in suits mixing and mastering engineers, and it works across stereo, surround, and Dolby Atmos sessions. For mastering work, 3 dB and 6 dB display ranges give a finer view of subtle adjustments, while 12 dB and 30 dB ranges are better suited to the broader moves typical of mixing.

On the sound side, Spectral dynamics allows problem frequencies to be addressed more precisely and efficiently than with standard EQ moves. The plug-in's Dynamic EQ includes Attack and Release controls along with optional free side-chain filtering, giving detailed control over how the dynamic processing responds. Improved dynamic EQ processing also results in less distortion and a cleaner overall sound, and this holds true across its zero-latency, Natural Phase, and linear phase modes.

Compared to free alternatives that also offer dynamic EQ, this plug-in distinguishes itself through features that matter for detailed mix and mastering work: a per-node real-time spectrum display, a zero-latency linear phase mode, and full mid/side control on a per-band basis. These aren't minor conveniences — they directly affect the workflow for engineers doing precise, detail-oriented EQ work.

## How to choose

<!-- role:choose -->

Choosing among these tools comes down to which gap in your setup you're actually trying to fill. Someone starting from nothing and needing a synthesizer first should install Vital. Someone whose current gap is reverb should start with Valhalla Supermassive for ambient, long-tail sounds, then bring in Valhalla Room when more control over room size and early reflections is needed — the two handle different jobs rather than overlapping. A producer who wants a complete EQ and dynamics chain can combine TDR Nova, TDR Kotelnikov, and MJUC jr to cover dynamic EQ, mastering-style compression, and tube character together. Anyone working in EDM, bass music, or hyperpop typically keeps OTT installed as a default, adding Wavesfactory Trackspacer when separation between competing sounds — bass against kick, or vocal against pad — is needed without introducing audible pumping. For someone with more room in their budget, Phase Plant stands out as the synthesis tool with the longest runway, since its modular layout scales with a user's skill in ways that fixed-architecture synthesizers do not.

Q: Are free VST plugins good enough for professional production in 2026?
A: For synthesis, equalization, dynamics processing, and reverb work, free plugins are often good enough without reservation. Vital, TDR Nova, and Valhalla Supermassive have each turned up in commercially released mixes. The real distinction in 2026 is not free versus paid, but purpose-built versus generic: a free plugin designed for one specific job frequently outperforms a pricier plugin used outside the task it was built for.

Q: Is Vital actually comparable to Xfer Serum?
A: Vital's free version includes a wavetable synthesis engine in the same class as the one most producers associate with Serum, and it is available at no cost.

Q: Do I need Valhalla Room if I already have Valhalla Supermassive?
A: The two plugins complement each other rather than duplicate one another. Supermassive is built for ambient, expansive, long-tail reverb. Room instead handles natural room simulation — small rooms, chambers, plates, and halls — with finer control over early reflections and decay characteristics. Many producers start with Supermassive alone and add Room later, once they notice that specific gap in their toolkit.

Q: What is the practical difference between TDR Nova and FabFilter Pro-Q 4?
A: Nova provides strong dynamic equalization at no cost and is a reasonable starting point. Pro-Q 4 adds a real-time per-band spectrum display, a zero-latency linear phase mode, and full mid/side control on each band. These extra features matter mainly for mastering and detailed mix-level EQ correction, and matter less for basic tracking and arranging. A sensible approach is to begin with Nova and move up only once a specific workflow limitation becomes clear.

Q: Should plugins be bought as bundles or one at a time on a limited budget?
A: Buying individual plugins within a defined budget tends to work out better than buying a bundle. Bundles lower the cost per plugin but often include tools that sit unused for a long time. Within a modest budget, a focused reverb plugin, a delay plugin, and a sidechain-aware dynamics plugin together can stay under that budget while solving everyday production problems directly. A small set of purpose-built tools tends to outperform a bundle where most of the content goes untouched.
