## Three parallel grain engines drive BEAM 2.0's effect stack
<!-- role:product -->

BEAM organizes its processing as separate nodes — convolution reverb, granular processing, filtering, and the newer Haze module — that can run together or on their own. The convolution reverb module draws on more than 200 impulse responses spanning halls, synthetic textures, and metallic spaces; the maker says its curated set includes sound design contributed by Benn Jordan and Venus Theory. The granular module runs up to three parallel grain engines at once, built for layered grain textures, glitch-edged rhythms, and evolving soundscapes. Haze, new in version 2.0, is a chorus and phase module adding algorithmic reverb and dispersion, alongside 80 new impulse responses and 60 new node presets. Every module doubles as a standalone plugin, and BEAM ships with four of them — Grains, Space, Filter, and Haze. Modulation runs through three LFOs and a Macro control, while an Intelligent Preset Designer generates new patches from a probability cluster algorithm. BEAM also expands by loading any Lunacy effect directly into its engine, with a library of more than 300 presets credited to designers such as Venus Theory, J.Views, Dash Glitch, Databroth, and Alckemy Neuro.

## Where BEAM 2.0's effects fit in a mix
<!-- role:audience -->

- **Vocal textures** — BEAM's nodes are positioned to "spice up" vocals, reshaping them with modular reverb and grain processing.
- **Drum reshaping** — The same engine is built to reshape drums by routing them through its granular or filter modules.
- **Synth and key layers** — Granular and modulation tools extend synth and key sounds, pushed through grain engines and LFO-driven movement.
- **Ambient and metallic spaces** — The convolution reverb module is built to wrap audio in "metal chains or a vast ambient space."

## How BEAM 2.0's modular stack compares with other Plugin Boutique effects
<!-- role:peers -->

Baby Audio's HyperWarp is also billed as a time-bending granular effect plugin for creating motion, texture, and color, putting it in similar territory to BEAM's grain engines. BEAM, though, runs its granular module as one node alongside convolution reverb, filtering, and the Haze chorus-reverb module, rather than as a single standalone granular processor. Heritage Audio's 73 JR Preamp sits at the other end of the spectrum: a single-purpose 73-style preamp offering up to 80dB of gain in 5dB steps, a narrower scope than BEAM's multi-node playground. ChordAXE is similarly single-purpose, letting players reach chord voicings beyond physical hand reach rather than processing audio the way BEAM's reverb and granular modules do.
