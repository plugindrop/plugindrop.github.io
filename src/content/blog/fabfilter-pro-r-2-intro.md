## What Pro-R 2 Does And How You Shape It
<!-- role:intro -->
<!-- pick:FabFilter Pro-R 2 -->

Pro-R 2 is FabFilter's reverb plug-in, built to add space around a track. It supports immersive formats up to 9.1.6 Dolby Atmos, so the reverb can sit correctly in surround and object-based mixes as well as plain stereo ones.

At its center is the Decay Rate EQ, which lets you shape how long different frequencies ring out using parametric bands spread across the spectrum. A Space control moves from tiny rooms to vast halls, and decay time adjusts to match the size you pick.

Ducking control for effortless separation of dry and wet signals lowers the reverb while the source plays, then lets it return once the source stops. Thickness control for dialing in program-dependent density and saturation adds body to the tail. An Intelligent Auto Gate cleans up the reverb's ending, and Output level meters let you watch levels while you print the effect.

## Shaping the Space: How Pro-R 2's Controls Build the Reverb
<!-- role:character -->

Pro-R 2 brings together a handful of controls that shape the final sound of a reverb. Space sets the room size, moving from tiny boxes to vast arenas and matching decay time to the chosen room model, so you can move from a tight space to a wide hall. Decay Rate EQ shapes the reverb's decay across the frequency spectrum with parametric EQ bands. Thickness control adds program-dependent density and saturation to the tail, building a fuller reverb without changing the underlying room. Ducking control lowers the wet signal while the dry signal is present, so the reverb steps back during busier passages and returns once things settle. Used together, these controls suit drums and vocals, and mixes that call for long, bright ambiences.

## Dialing in a plate sound for drums and vocals
<!-- role:workflow -->

Say a producer wants a drum bus or a vocal take to sit in a reverb that feels like a classic metallic plate rather than a room or a hall. That is the situation Plate is built for in Pro-R 2, giving drums and vocals that evocative plate character without reaching for outboard gear. From there, the goal is to keep that reverb full without it turning muddy, so Thickness helps add body and density to the tail. Ducking keeps the wet signal out of the way while the dry hit or word is still sounding, so the plate effect does not smear the front of each hit or syllable. And since a plate character can still range from a tight slap to a bigger wash, Space lets the producer size that ambience to fit the drum or vocal moment without losing the plate identity.

## Pro-R 2 Reverb Questions
<!-- role:faq -->

Q: What does the Decay Rate EQ let you control in the reverb tail?

A: It adds parametric EQ bands across the decay, so you can let certain frequency ranges ring on while others fade faster, rather than the whole tail decaying at one rate. You can combine that with Space, which scales from tiny rooms to vast arenas and matches decay time to whichever room you pick. Vintage and Plate are separate character options for the reverb itself. Vintage leans toward long, bright, hyper-real ambience, while Plate brings a metallic texture suited to drums and vocals.

Q: How do Thickness and Ducking change the way the reverb sits in a mix?

A: Thickness adds program-dependent density, saturation, and compression to the reverb, making it feel more substantial without altering the room character you have chosen. Ducking works differently. It lowers the wet reverb whenever the original, un-reverberated sound is active, so the two do not compete for space. An Intelligent Auto Gate and output level meters round out the practical side, giving you a way to tame lingering tails automatically and keep track of overall output level.
