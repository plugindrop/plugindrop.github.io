That shift is the real starting point for understanding VST plugins today.

The free plugins covered in this guide are used in real, finished productions, not just practice sessions — free tools have earned a permanent place alongside paid ones rather than serving as a temporary substitute for them.

## FAQ

<!-- role:faq -->

Q: Do I need a DAW installed before VST plugins will work?
A: Yes. VST plugins are instruments and effects that run inside a host application rather than as standalone programs. Ableton Live, FL Studio, Logic Pro (for Audio Units on macOS), Reaper, and Studio One all support VST3 and AU formats. Install a DAW first, then add plugins to it.

Q: What is the difference between VST, VST3, and AU?
A: VST2 is the original plugin format and is now considered legacy, since most developers have stopped releasing new VST2 versions. VST3 is the current cross-platform standard for Windows and macOS. AU, short for Audio Units, is the format used natively by Logic Pro on macOS and iOS. The free plugins covered in this guide ship in both VST3 and AU builds, covering every major DAW in current use.

Q: Will these free plugins work on Apple Silicon Macs (M1/M2/M3/M4)?
A: The free plugins covered in this guide offer native Apple Silicon builds, with ARM-native versions published for each. Rosetta 2 compatibility exists as a fallback for older plugin versions, but native ARM builds are available and recommended.

Q: How many plugins do I actually need to get started?
A: Four categories cover the essentials: one synthesizer, one EQ, one compressor (a DAW's built-in compressor is sufficient to start), and one reverb. Free tools can cover most of these categories at no cost. Accumulating plugins faster than you can learn them is a common beginner mistake.

Q: Is a free synth tier actually free or is it a time-limited trial?
A: A free tier is not necessarily a trial. It can include a full synthesis engine, with multiple oscillators, a complete modulation matrix, a wavetable editor, and a set of included presets, with no time limit or expiration. A paid upgrade tier may add online preset packs, while the core synthesis capability remains fully unlocked in the free version.
