## What you actually get

Celemony lists Melodyne 5's core technology as Grammy Award-winning, built around a note-based way of working rather than raw waveform editing. That means you select and drag individual notes to fix pitch or timing, instead of manually drawing automation curves.

According to the product listing, the engine runs on three algorithms: Melodic (with Sibilant Detection), Percussive, and Universal. Choosing the right one for a vocal, a drum hit, or mixed material should keep artifacts down during correction.

- Main Tool for direct pitch and timing edits on detected notes.
- ARA (Audio Random Access) integration, depending on the host DAW, for inline editing without bouncing audio back and forth.
- New in Melodyne 5: the Melodic algorithm can edit pitched and noise-like components of a sound separately.

That last point matters for breathy or sibilant vocal takes, where the noise component often needs different handling than the tonal pitch.

## Where it fits in a session

Melodyne sits naturally after a vocal or solo-instrument take is recorded, before mix bus processing. Pull a take in via ARA, fix tuning and timing note by note, then send the corrected audio downstream to EQ and compression. It also works as a timing-alignment tool for doubled vocal takes or simple monophonic parts that drifted off the grid.
