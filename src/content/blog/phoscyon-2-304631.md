## Phoscyon 2 Rebuilds The 303 Signal Path From Scratch
<!-- role:product -->
D16 redesigned Phoscyon 2 from its predecessor, Phoscyon 1, rebuilding the plugin's signal path to emulate the Roland TB-303 "silver box" synthesizer with a higher degree of accuracy. A sequencer is built into the interface for programming patterns directly in the plugin. Beyond the synthesis knobs found on the original 303, Phoscyon 2 exposes Calibration Parameters and the service trim pots originally used to calibrate individual hardware units, which let a project replicate the character differences between individual 303 units. The effects chain adds a 4-band parametric EQ, a delay with tempo sync and an in-loop multi-mode filter, a chorus with two delay lines, an algorithmic reverb with independent early and late reflection settings, and a limiter with controllable knee, ratio, threshold and envelope. Over 800 factory presets and patterns ship with the plugin, the GUI is re-sizable across several size options, and MIDI Learn maps on-screen controls to an external MIDI controller. ## The Tone Calibration Parameters Dial In
<!-- role:sound -->
Phoscyon 2's vibrato effect is built from two alternating notes rather than an oscillator: the spread between the two notes sets the vibrato range, and their duration sets its speed, reproducing with two knobs a trick historically achieved through two-note sliding. The distortion stage carries 19 distinctive-sounding models based on hardware units associated with well-known 303 artists, and effect order in the chain can be reshuffled to change the resulting tone. D16 describes the calibration-trim access as "a backstage pass to the inner workings of the synth," letting a project replicate the distinctive character individual 303 hardware units are known for.

## Three Ways Producers Put Phoscyon 2 To Work
<!-- role:audience -->
- **Acid bassline programming** — the built-in sequencer lets users build 303-style patterns directly in the plugin.
- **Hardware-matched calibration** — Calibration Parameters and service trim pots replicate the distinctive cutoff and tone variations of individual original 303 units.
- **Hands-on performance control** — MIDI Learn maps the plugin's controls to an external MIDI controller for real-time adjustment.

## FAQ
<!-- role:faq -->
Q: What changed between the original Phoscyon and Phoscyon 2?
A: D16 redesigned the signal path for higher emulation accuracy and added Calibration Parameters and service trim pots that expose per-unit character differences absent from the first version.

Q: How is Phoscyon 2's vibrato controlled?
A: Vibrato range comes from the spread between its two alternating notes, and speed comes from their duration, both adjustable with two dedicated knobs independent of presets.

Q: What does MIDI Learn do in Phoscyon 2?
A: MIDI Learn links almost any visible control in the plugin to an external MIDI controller, allowing controls to be mapped for hands-on adjustment.
