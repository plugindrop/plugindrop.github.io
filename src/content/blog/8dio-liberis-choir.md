## How Liberis Fits a Choir Toolkit

Liberis has no direct rival in 8Dio's catalog, so a spec-sheet comparison against competing products isn't the useful exercise. The practical question is what job it does in a template.

### What the specs suggest

- **Multiple mic positions** let you place the choir close and intimate for a single exposed line, or push toward the hall mic for a distant, sacral wash.
- **Layered legato and multi-vowel phrases** matter for sustained writing. Held "ah" and "oo" lines with smooth dynamic movement are the typical use case for a choir library, and variety in vowels reduces obvious repetition across a cue.
- **Separate soloists** give you a way to open a cue with one voice and bring the ensemble in underneath or after.
- **The effects set** is aimed at sound design: breathy consonants, textural hits, and vowel fragments that can be layered into pads or risers.

### Pairing with an adult choir

On its own, Liberis covers one narrow emotional register. Stacked with an adult library such as Requiem Light or Voice of Rapture, it becomes part of a wider palette: the adult choir supplies weight and the children's choir adds the high, bright top layer. This is the main reason the bundle structure makes sense.

### Practical layering examples

- **Trailer build:** adult choir carries the low-mid power chords while Liberis doubles the top line at a lower level for an eerie shimmer.
- **Sacred underscore:** soloist patch states the melody, full ensemble enters on the repeat with the hall mic emphasized.
- **Game flashback cue:** Liberis alone over a soft pad, with close mic dominant and minimal reverb for an intimate, memory-like sound.
- **Hybrid texture:** blend the effects patches under strings and synths as an additional airy layer.
