## Quad-Engine Layers Meet an XY Pad
<!-- role:product -->

Four sound sources—A, B, C, and D—form the core of Evolve Elastic Lite. The instrument lets producers blend four layers of samples or synths, then move between them with an XY Pad. In plain terms, it is designed for combining several sound elements and changing their balance from one control surface instead of treating every source as an isolated instrument.

The quad-engine includes sample and synth OSC engines, with 125 supplied sound sources spanning Chords, FX, Impacts, Instruments, Multisample, Synth, and Textures. A Sounds Browser provides access to those sources, while 75 presets offer ready-made starting points. One-click Randomise Sounds can generate a new combination without manually selecting each layer.

The XY Pad operates in Sounds and FX modes. Its sound controls include pitch, fine tuning, tone, pan, velocity sensitivity, volume, playback direction, and keytracking. Synth-oriented controls include an oscillator shape slider with Sine, Triangle, Saw, Square, and Pulse options, plus unison, detune, 1–32 voices, legato, glide time, pitch bend, and global pitch. Macro routing can run pre- or post-FX, and Eco Mode is available.

## Melt, Bounce, Stretch, and Gloss Define Its Character
<!-- role:sound -->

Melt, Bounce, Stretch, and Gloss are Evolve Elastic Lite’s four elastic-inspired macro effects. They are the dedicated output-shaping controls after the four layers have been blended, giving the instrument a direct route from a layered source selection to a changed result.

Its stated sound character is futuristic, playful, artificial, and unpredictable, with sounds that morph into unexpected forms. The supplied preset direction is tied to experimental electronic artists including Flume, SOPHIE, and Aphex Twin. That lineage frames Evolve Elastic Lite around experimental electronic sound design rather than a narrowly fixed instrument type.

The combination matters: the XY Pad can morph Sounds or FX in real time, while the four macros reshape the output. Randomisation adds another way to reach unexpected forms. For producers seeking synth material that can be assembled from samples or synth oscillators and then transformed through macro controls, those are the central tools.
