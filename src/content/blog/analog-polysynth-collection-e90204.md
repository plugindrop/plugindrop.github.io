## JUPITER-8 and JUNO-106 Recreated With ACB Modeling
<!-- role:product -->

Five synthesizer models from the late 1970s and early 1980s—the JUPITER-4, JUPITER-8, JUNO-60, JX-3P, and JUNO-106—make up this bundle for VST3, AU, and AAX hosts, with native Apple silicon support. Each instrument runs on Analog Circuit Behavior (ACB) technology, Roland's process of analyzing original hardware units and circuit diagrams to recreate how the circuits actually behaved.

Detailed interfaces mirror the layout of the vintage units, and the collection adds extended parameters, an advanced arpeggiator, and onboard effects beyond what the original hardware offered; some models also gain increased polyphony over the hardware they're modeled on. Patches include the original factory sounds plus new presets built for contemporary production. The bundle also carries PLUG-OUT compatibility, letting patches and parameter changes move between the plug-ins and Roland's SYSTEM-8 hardware synth.

Where u-he's Zebra 3 ships with over 1,200 factory presets and 20 oscillator effects such as spectral decay and phase remapping, Analog Polysynth Collection instead centers on five specific hardware recreations with their original patch sets rather than a broad synthesis effects toolkit. ## The Organic Response ACB Modeling Carries Over
<!-- role:sound -->

According to Roland, the five instruments "sound and behave exactly like the original hardware with stunning accuracy." That accuracy comes from modeling each original unit's circuit behavior, including its quirks and instabilities, rather than just its tone at a single setting. The result carries over the lifelike sound and organic response associated with the original analog circuitry, rather than a cleaned-up digital approximation. That same modeling also means some patches respond less predictably than a modern digital synth would—the instability is part of what the ACB process is built to preserve.
