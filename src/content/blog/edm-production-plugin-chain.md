## Introduction

<!-- role:intro -->

FabFilter Pro-Q 4 is a parametric EQ plug-in that supports up to 24 bands of adjustment. What sets it apart is its Spectral dynamics feature, which lets producers target and treat problem frequencies with more precision and efficiency than a standard EQ band allows.

The plug-in is built for both mixing and mastering work, and it handles stereo, surround, and Dolby Atmos sessions. For mastering tasks, 3 dB and 6 dB display ranges give a finer, more detailed view of the frequency curve, while mixing engineers can switch to wider 12 dB and 30 dB ranges to shape broader tonal changes.

Beyond static EQ, Pro-Q 4 brings dynamic processing into the same tool. Spectral dynamics addresses problem frequencies more cleanly, and Dynamic EQ bands offer adjustable Attack and Release times along with an optional free side-chain filter, so the EQ reacts to the signal rather than staying fixed. Updated dynamic EQ processing also reduces distortion, keeping the sound cleaner whether working in zero-latency, Natural Phase, or linear phase mode.

## Step 5: Master Bus Limiting — Hit Streaming Targets Without Destroying the Drop

The master bus limiter is the final stage in the chain and the most consequential for how your track translates on streaming platforms. Under-limiting leaves loudness on the table. Over-limiting destroys transients and makes the drop feel flat. FabFilter Pro-L 2 is a limiter built for transparent, streaming-ready loudness processing. The real-time loudness meter reads LUFS-I (integrated), LUFS-S (short-term), and true peak simultaneously, which eliminates the need for a separate metering plugin at the mastering stage. **Best for:** Final master bus limiting to streaming platform targets — -14 LUFS integrated for Spotify, -16 LUFS for YouTube — with true peak compliance.

Serum's wavetable system also lets you load and edit presets from third-party sound designers. The Total Bundle adds Pro-C 3 (compressor), Pro-MB (multiband dynamics), Saturn 2 (saturation and distortion), Pro-DS (de-esser), Volcano 3 (filter), and the complete synthesizer suite — meaning every processing stage in this chain can be handled by FabFilter tools with consistent metering, UI behavior, and inter-plugin spectrum sharing.

For producers who want to replace OTT with a more transparent multiband compressor and add Saturn 2 as a saturation stage between synthesis and EQ, the bundle is the most cost-efficient path. Start at 30–40% Depth and work up. - **If you are spending hours searching for the right lead sound,** Serum's wavetable architecture and preset browser give you starting points and the tools to understand how sounds are built. - **If your mix has frequency buildup in the 200–500 Hz range after OTT,** FabFilter Pro-Q 4's dynamic EQ mode will address it more accurately than a static notch — the plugin only cuts when that frequency range is active, which preserves body when the signal is not congested. - **If your master is hitting streaming loudness normalization inconsistently,** Pro-L 2's per-platform LUFS presets remove the guesswork — set the integrated loudness target, engage true peak limiting, and the limiter handles the translation. - **If budget is the primary constraint,** OTT and Valhalla Supermassive are both free with no registration. ---

FabFilter Pro-L 2 is a mastering-grade limiter built for engineers who need both transparent sound and maximum loudness without sacrificing clarity. ## FAQ

<!-- role:faq -->

Q: Do I need all five plugins to build a usable EDM chain?
A: No. OTT and Valhalla Supermassive are both free, so the only required purchase is Serum. A track built with Serum, OTT, Supermassive, and a digital audio workstation's built-in EQ and limiter is already a competitive starting chain. Pro-Q 4 and Pro-L 2 become the next logical purchases once the built-in tools become the audible limitation, typically at the mix bus EQ and mastering stages.

Q: Where exactly does OTT go in the signal chain?
A: In this chain, OTT sits after sound design and before EQ, typically applied to individual synth tracks or a group bus for the drop elements. Some producers add a second instance at a reduced depth setting on the full mix bus for cohesion, though this is more genre-dependent and less universal than applying it per track.

Q: Is Valhalla Supermassive only useful for large reverbs?
A: No. Its shorter delay modes work well for chorus, doubling, and tight room effects, not only long, spacious tails. The name describing huge reverb sizes describes the upper ceiling of what the algorithm can do, not the minimum setting it has to be used at.

Q: Can Pro-L 2 replace a dedicated metering plugin?
A: Yes, for streaming-focused self-mastering. Its built-in integrated loudness, short-term loudness, and true peak metering covers the measurement needs for major streaming platforms without requiring a separate plugin. Mastering engineers handling client delivery reports often still add a dedicated meter, but for bedroom production the built-in metering is sufficient and removes one plugin from the master bus chain.

Q: What is the difference between buying Serum outright versus through a rental platform?
A: Serum can be bought outright as a single one-time purchase, or obtained through third-party rental-to-own platforms that charge a smaller recurring payment that accumulates toward the full license. The plugin itself is identical either way. Buying outright transfers ownership of the license right away, while the rental option spreads out the cost but takes longer before the license is fully owned.
