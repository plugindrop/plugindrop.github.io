## Five Roland Hardware Synths Rebuilt With ACB Modeling
<!-- role:product -->

Roland built the Analog Monosynth Collection as a bundle of five of its own late-'70s and early-'80s monosynths: the SH-101, SH-2, TB-303, SYSTEM-100, and PROMARS. Each is recreated with Analog Circuit Behavior (ACB) technology, Roland's circuit-modeling process that analyzes the original hardware units, their circuit diagrams, and other historical data to rebuild how each synth's circuitry actually behaves rather than just approximating its sound.

The interfaces are detailed enough to give the feel of working with the original hardware, while extended parameters, an onboard arpeggiator, and built-in effects add workflow options the vintage units never had. Every instrument carries its original factory patches plus new presets aimed at contemporary production. For studios built around Roland's SYSTEM-8 hardware synth, PLUG-OUT compatibility lets any of the five run directly on that hardware instead of staying confined to a DAW.

The bundle installs as VST3, AU, and AAX plugins with native Apple silicon support. By comparison, Roland's standalone JUNO-106 plugin takes a different approach entirely: it's a single synth with adjustable polyphony from two to eight notes and a Circuit Mod function for tweaking its sonic character in real time, rather than five separate vintage monosynths bundled together.

## The Vintage Quirks ACB Modeling Keeps Intact
<!-- role:sound -->

The sound character here comes directly from how the ACB process works: Roland studies original circuit behavior down to its flaws, then rebuilds those same flaws in software instead of smoothing them out. Per Roland's own description, the result is that these recreations "sound and behave exactly like the original hardware with stunning accuracy." That accuracy extends to the instabilities themselves — the modeling carries over the vintage instrument's quirks and drift rather than cleaning them up, which is what gives each of the five synths its lifelike, organic response instead of a sanitized approximation.

In practice, that means the SH-101's and TB-303's circuit-level behavior, SYSTEM-100's and PROMARS's included, arrives with the same organic unpredictability the original hardware had, not a quantized emulation of it.
