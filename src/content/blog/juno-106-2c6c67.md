## A DCO-Based Polysynth Rebuilt With ACB Circuit Modeling
<!-- role:product -->

Roland built the JUNO-106 Software Synthesizer as a plug-in reconstruction of the 1984 hardware polysynth, using its ACB process to analyze the original circuit diagrams and historical component data. Roland says the result reproduces "the authentic circuit behaviors of the vintage instrument, including all the beloved quirks and instabilities," rather than a generic emulation. The plug-in keeps the source machine's single-DCO-plus-sub-oscillator architecture and its characterful filter, then adds velocity sensitivity, an extended oscillator range, a second envelope, and an onboard arpeggiator that were not part of the original design. Polyphony is adjustable from two to eight notes, and a Circuit Mod function lets a user reshape the sonic character in real time. Effects include the chorus alongside reverb, flanger, delay, and overdrive, and an integrated browser searches across every installed patch bank, mixing classic patches with newly written ones. The plug-in also offers PLUG-OUT compatibility with the SYSTEM-8 hardware synth, runs as VST3, AU, or AAX, and carries native Apple silicon support inside a resizable, high-definition interface.

## The Tone Its Chorus and Sub-Oscillator Are Built Around
<!-- role:sound -->

A single DCO paired with a fat sub-oscillator gives the JUNO-106 its low-end weight, while the lush chorus circuit widens pads and plucked lines into the swirling character the hardware became known for. That combination produces gut-punching bass, delicate plucked tones, and shimmering pads without heavy sound design, since the architecture stays deliberately simple.
