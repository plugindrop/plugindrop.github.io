## What you actually get

The JUNO-106 is a software recreation of Roland's 1984 analog polysynth, one of the most sampled and imitated synths in electronic music history. The original hardware built its sound around DCO-based oscillators paired with a resonant low-pass filter, plus a switchable chorus circuit (Chorus I/II) that gave it that signature wide, shimmering character.

The software version aims to model that same signal path in a plugin format for DAW use. Expect the familiar single-oscillator-per-voice architecture, the chorus modes, and a straightforward, hands-on control layout that mirrors the hardware panel. For exact preset counts, polyphony limits, and plugin formats, check the product page for details.

## Where it fits in a session

This is a synth for parts where the chorus does the work: 80s-style pads, sustained analog strings, and basslines that need width without layering multiple tracks. It also holds up for retro house and synth-pop leads where a slightly imperfect, warm oscillator sound is the goal rather than pristine digital accuracy.

Producers building house, disco, synthwave, or lo-fi analog textures will find the most direct use for it. It's less suited as a primary sound-design engine for modern bass or FM-style patches — that's not the niche it's modeling.
