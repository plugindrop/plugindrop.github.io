## A single-DCO architecture rebuilt with Roland's ACB modeling
<!-- role:product -->

Roland built the JUNO-106 Software Synthesizer around the same formula that defined the original 1984 hardware: a single DCO, a sub-oscillator, and a resonant VCF filter, feeding into a chorus circuit. Roland's ACB circuit-modeling process studied the original hardware's circuit diagrams and components to reproduce its circuit behavior, including quirks and instabilities particular to that unit. On top of that recreation, Roland added velocity sensitivity, an extended oscillator range, a second envelope, and an onboard arpeggiator, none of which were part of the vintage original. Polyphony is adjustable from two to eight notes, and a Circuit Mod function allows real-time tweaking of the instrument's sonic character. The LFO, arpeggiator, and onboard effects — among them reverb, flanger, delay, and overdrive alongside the chorus — sync to host tempo. An integrated browser searches across installed patch banks, covering both classic patches and newly created sounds. The plug-in runs as VST3, AU, or AAX with a resizable, high-definition interface, and it supports PLUG-OUT operation with the SYSTEM-8 hardware synth. Roland separately bundles the JUNO-106 inside the Analog Polysynth Collection alongside recreations of the JUPITER-4, JUPITER-8, JUNO-60, and JX-3P, where the standalone plug-in only covers the JUNO-106 model. Full specifications are listed on the [JUNO-106 Software Synthesizer product page](https://www.pluginboutique.com/search?q=JUNO+106&a_aid=69cb95abe1763&chan=art&data1=juno-106-juno-1&utm_source=plugindrop&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=juno-106-juno-1).
