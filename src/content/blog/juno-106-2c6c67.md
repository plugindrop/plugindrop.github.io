## ACB Circuit Modeling Rebuilds a 1984 Polysynth
<!-- role:product -->

The JUNO-106 Software Synthesizer is Roland's plug-in version of the polysynth it first sold in 1984 as an affordable option for a wider range of players. Roland recreates the instrument using its ACB (Analog Circuit Behavior) process, which analyzes the original circuit diagrams and hardware units and then reproduces how those circuits actually behaved, including the quirks and instabilities of the vintage design. The underlying architecture pairs one DCO with a sub-oscillator and a characterful filter, routed through the chorus circuit that the original hardware was known for. Roland added a handful of modern additions on top of that core design: velocity sensitivity, an extended oscillator range, a second envelope, and an onboard arpeggiator, with the LFO, arpeggiator, and effects all syncing to host tempo. Polyphony is adjustable from two to eight notes, a Circuit Mod function allows real-time tweaking of the sonic character, and an integrated browser searches across every installed patch bank, including the classic presets and newly added sounds. The plug-in also offers PLUG-OUT compatibility with Roland's SYSTEM-8 hardware and runs as VST3, AU, or AAX with native Apple silicon support.

## A Fat Sub-Oscillator Behind the Legendary Chorus
<!-- role:sound -->

The sound comes down to that simple DCO-plus-sub-oscillator-plus-filter formula, which Roland describes as producing a fat low end, paired with a chorus effect it calls lush. Because the ACB modeling targets the authentic circuit behavior of the original unit, including its beloved quirks and instabilities, the plug-in carries forward the same character rather than a cleaned-up approximation of it. On top of the core engine, the plug-in also includes reverb, flanger, delay, and overdrive, so the chorus-driven base tone can be shaped further without leaving the instrument.
