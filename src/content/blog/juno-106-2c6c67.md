## The ACB Circuit Modeling Driving This JUNO-106 Recreation
<!-- role:product -->

Roland built the JUNO-106 Software Synthesizer around ACB technology, its method of modeling original hardware by analyzing circuit diagrams and actual units rather than sampling them. The result reproduces the single DCO, sub-oscillator, and VCF structure of the 1984 JUNO-106, along with the quirks and instabilities that came from the original's analog manufacturing tolerances. Roland added capabilities the 1984 hardware never had: velocity sensitivity, an extended oscillator range, a second envelope, and an onboard arpeggiator that syncs to tempo alongside the LFO and effects. Onboard effects extend beyond the signature chorus to include reverb, flanger, delay, and overdrive. A Circuit Mod function lets a producer adjust the modeled circuit's sonic character in real time, and polyphony is adjustable from two to eight notes. The plug-in runs as VST3, AU, or AAX with native Apple silicon support, and it also works as a PLUG-OUT controller for the SYSTEM-8 hardware synth, moving patches between the software and that keyboard. An integrated browser searches across every installed patch bank, spanning the included classic patches and newly added sounds. ## The Tone Built Around a Single DCO and Chorus
<!-- role:sound -->

The JUNO formula pairs a single DCO with what Roland calls a "fat" sub-oscillator and a characterful VCF, topped by a chorus circuit the company describes as "lush." That chorus swirl is the signature element of the JUNO-106 sound, shaping the gut-punching basses, delicate plucked tones, and shimmering pads the architecture produces. ACB modeling carries over the authentic circuit behavior of the vintage instrument, including the quirks and instabilities that gave original units their character, so the software version keeps the same swirling, slightly unstable analog quality rather than a cleaned-up approximation. The onboard reverb, flanger, delay, and overdrive sit alongside that chorus, giving the modeled signal extra space without leaving the plug-in's modeled circuit path.
