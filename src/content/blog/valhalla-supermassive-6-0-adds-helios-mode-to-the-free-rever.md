## Where it fits in a session

Supermassive works well as a send effect on a reverb/delay bus, where you can blend wet signal under vocals, synths, or guitars. It's also a common choice for sound design passes, where producers push the algorithms into long, smeared textures that sit behind a mix rather than sitting on top of it.

With a new mode added, Helios gives sound designers and ambient producers another variation to audition before committing to a final reverb or delay chain. It's worth trying on pads, risers, and transition elements where texture matters more than precise tail control.

## Skip it if

Skip this update if you need tightly controlled, studio-reference-grade reverb tails for close mic work, since Supermassive leans toward generative and textural results rather than surgical room simulation. If you're not already a Supermassive user, there's little reason to install it just for Helios alone — it's additive to an existing free tool, not a standalone reason to switch workflows.

If your current session already relies on a dedicated algorithmic reverb for mix-bus duties, Helios is unlikely to replace that role. It's an extra flavor inside a free utility, not a mastering-grade reverb engine.
