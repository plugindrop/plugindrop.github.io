FM8 is a frequency-modulation synthesizer built around an operator matrix that lets players connect individual operators to one another and control how strongly each one modulates the next. Frequency modulation itself works through this interaction of operators, and a Morph Square feature lets a sound shift gradually between four different timbres, adding movement beyond a static patch.

The instrument covers a wide range of musical roles. Splitting the keyboard lets one hand trigger arpeggiator patterns while the other plays chords or bass lines at the same time, which makes it useful for live bass work and rhythmic playing. Its bells and keys sounds are classic and crystalline, suited to melodic and percussive playing of high quality. On the brass and piano side, the engine is rich and detailed, moving from dynamic digital pianos to bright, bold brass tones that show off the strengths of FM synthesis. Overall, the sound character leans bright, with that same richness and detail carrying across its different sound categories.

A substantial library comes bundled with the instrument: more than 1200 expertly designed presets, twelve high-quality effects covering a broad range of processing needs, and a set of extras including effect racks, sound morphing tools, and arpeggio presets.

It suits producers who want melodic or percussive keys, digital pianos, brass, and bass sounds, as well as keyboard players who like combining arpeggiator patterns with chords or basslines during performance.

On the system side, Windows users need Windows 10 or 11 with the latest service pack, 64-bit only, along with 4 GB of RAM, though 6 GB is recommended. Mac users need macOS 13, 14, or 15 with the latest update, running natively on Apple Silicon, also 64-bit only.

Measured against other FM-capable instruments on the market, including Atoms, KV331 Audio's SynthMaster 3, and Moog's Mariana, this synthesizer stands out as a dedicated FM workhorse backed by a large, ready-made preset library and built-in effects processing.
