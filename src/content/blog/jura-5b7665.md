## A Single-DCO Synth With a Switchable Front Panel
<!-- role:product -->
AIR Music Technology recreates a 1982 Japanese synthesizer architecture inside Jura, built around a single DCO, a sub oscillator, and the VCF filter section that defined the original hardware. The plugin ships for AU, VST, and AAX hosts, plus standalone MPC/FORCE versions that add full touch control over every parameter for producers working on qualified AKAI hardware. Two workflows sit side by side: the original front-panel layout for players who know the hardware, and an expanded modern interface built on top of it for deeper editing. Underneath both panels, AIR added features absent from the 1982 original — a second ADSR envelope, two DCO noise types (modern and vintage), and saturation and drive stages inside the VCF — so the oscillator and filter section can push past the vintage circuit's original limits.

## The Tone Built Around the Chorus and Sub Oscillator
<!-- role:sound -->
The 1982 architecture Jura recreates found its way onto records by The Eurythmics, Cyndi Lauper, and Aha, and the same design turns up more recently on tracks by Charlie Puth, Tame Impala, and the Weeknd. That lineage runs through the single DCO and sub oscillator, which drive a heavy analog low end, and the Juno-style chorus section, which thickens pads and leads with the detune the original hardware was known for. A delay (up to two seconds, BPM syncable), a reverb section, and a 3-band EQ sit after the filter, so the chorus-driven tone can be shaped further without leaving the plugin.

## How Jura's Filter Section Sits Next to Cherry Audio's Synths
<!-- role:peers -->
Cherry Audio's GX-80 targets a different lineage, recreating the CS-80 and GX-1 filters rather than the single-DCO, sub-oscillator design in Jura. Cherry Audio's Crumar Spirit takes a different approach to voice count, switching between monophonic, unison, and polyphonic 4-, 8-, or 16-voice modes with over 430 presets, where Jura instead runs one DCO per voice behind its original and expanded front panels.
