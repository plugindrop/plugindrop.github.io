## What you actually get

AIR Music Technology lists an oscillator section covering pulse, saw, sub, and noise waveforms, which gives you the raw material for both basses and leads. The listing also includes pulse width modulation driven manually, by LFO, or by either of two envelope sources, useful for the classic Juno "movement" in pad sounds.

According to the product listing, the VCF filter section adds cutoff, resonance, saturation, and drive, with modulation available from an invertible envelope, LFO, keyboard tracking, and velocity. A separate HPF control and a VCA section with gate or envelope modes round out the signal path. Two ADSR envelopes handle the filter and amplitude stages.

The product page also lists a BPM-syncable LFO with eight shapes, including two random generators, plus speed, delay, and ramp controls that can be re-synced or triggered manually. On top of that sits a classic Juno chorus section and a delay section running up to two seconds, also BPM syncable.

## Where it fits in a session

The oscillator and filter combination is suited to layering basses and mono leads where saturation and drive matter as much as pitch. The Juno chorus section is the obvious move for stereo-widening pads or guitar-style synth lines during mixdown rather than at the tracking stage.

The BPM-synced LFO and delay make Jura useful for rhythmic modulation parts and sync'd delay textures, since both lock to your session tempo instead of requiring manual timing. Velocity and keyboard tracking on the filter also make it a reasonable pick for performance-driven parts rather than purely programmed ones.
