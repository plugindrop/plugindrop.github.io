## A Single DCO Feeds Jura's VCF Engine
<!-- role:product -->

AIR Music modeled Jura on a synth design that originated in 1982, and the brief credits that original circuit with appearances on records by The Eurythmics, Cyndi Lauper, and Aha, plus more recent use on tracks by Charlie Puth, Tame Impala, and the Weeknd. Jura's engine centers on a single DCO paired with a sub oscillator, feeding into a VCF built around cutoff and resonance controls. AIR Music frames the plugin as built for modern production workflows on top of that recreated circuit, per the brief, and backs that framing with two interface modes: an original front-panel layout and an extended UI with additional controls. Beyond the core oscillator and filter, Jura adds a second envelope not present on the 1982 original, VCF saturation and drive, and two selectable DCO noise types, modern and vintage. A delay, reverb, and 3-band EQ sit alongside the chorus circuit, and an arpeggiator rounds out the modulation options. The lineup spans an AU/VST/AAX plugin plus MPC/Force standalone versions, the latter giving qualified AKAI hardware full touch control over every parameter.

## The 1982 Tone Jura's DCO and Sub Oscillator Target
<!-- role:sound -->

Jura's single DCO and sub oscillator aim at the analogue tone of that same 1982 Japanese synth design, the circuit the brief ties to 1980s records by The Eurythmics, Cyndi Lauper, and Aha, and to newer tracks by Charlie Puth, Tame Impala, and the Weeknd. The chorus section carries that era-specific coloration forward, while the VCF's saturation and drive, plus the modern and vintage DCO noise types, push the same source tone further than the original 1982 circuit could reach on its own.

## Where Jura's Filter Section Sits Next to Cherry Audio's Synths
<!-- role:peers -->

Cherry Audio's GX-80 pursues a different filter lineage, built as a precision recreation of both the CS-80 and the GX-1's filters, where Jura instead centers on a single DCO feeding one VCF with cutoff, resonance, saturation, and drive. Voice count marks another split: Cherry Audio's Crumar Spirit offers monophonic, unison, and polyphonic modes running up to 16 voices with cycle voice switching, while Jura's brief describes detune-able unison and editable chord modes rather than a multi-voice polyphonic engine. On modulation shaping, Super-7 ships an advanced arpeggiator and phraser with editable parameters and a preset library, while Jura's own arpeggiator sits within a broader modulation set built around BPM-syncable LFOs with eight shapes, including two random generators.
