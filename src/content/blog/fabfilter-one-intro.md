**TL;DR:** FabFilter One is a self-oscillating low-pass filter plug-in built around an aliasing-free oscillator, aimed at producers who want its fat, self-oscillating low-pass filter as the centerpiece of a patch. It fits keys parts well thanks to a Hold setting that lets held notes sustain longer than usual, and it works for sound design through its LFO, which syncs to tempo and offers triangle and square waveforms with adjustable wave balance, alongside a fine-tuned envelope generator. Sound designers can shape tones further with frequency and filter cut-off modulation, pulse width modulation, and filter keyboard tracking, giving a compact but flexible set of controls for anyone building synth patches from scratch.

## Getting to Know FabFilter One's Filter and Modulation Controls
<!-- role:intro -->
<!-- pick:FabFilter One -->

FabFilter One is built around an aliasing-free oscillator and a Fat and ultra-smooth self-oscillating low-pass filter, and most of its controls exist to shape that filter. Frequency and filter cut-off modulation covers both pitch and filter movement, giving sound design work extra material to pull from. Pulse width modulation adds another layer for the same kind of exploratory patching. Movement across the instrument comes from an LFO with tempo sync, triangle and square waveforms, and adjustable wave balance. For playing keys, a Fine-tuned EG with additional Hold setting lets a note stay open past where it would normally release, so chords and pads keep sounding while you work elsewhere on the patch. Filter keyboard tracking keeps that same filter sitting consistently as you move up and down the keyboard. Together, these pieces make FabFilter One equally suited to playable keys sounds and more open-ended sound design.

## How FabFilter One's Controls Shape the Sound
<!-- role:character -->

FabFilter One pairs an aliasing-free oscillator with a fat and ultra-smooth self-oscillating low-pass filter, and that pairing sets the core character of every patch. Layered on top is an LFO with tempo sync, triangle and square waveforms, and adjustable wave balance, which adds motion that producers can shape to fit a track's tempo. A fine-tuned EG with additional Hold setting controls how a sound develops over time, and the Hold stage keeps a patch sustained for as long as a note is held. Pulse width modulation and filter keyboard tracking round out the set, giving extra ways to vary a patch. Each of these controls can be used on its own or combined, so a producer building keys sounds or working through sound design has several independent points to shape a patch from.

Picture a part where held chords need to breathe a little longer than your fingers can manage, or a patch that has to shift character as a track builds. FabFilter One fits both jobs from the same instrument. For keys parts, you can lean on the extended Hold behavior so a chord or line keeps ringing past the point where you lift your hands, which is handy when you want a pad or sustained figure to sit under a vocal or lead without re-triggering notes constantly. Used this way, FabFilter One works less like a one-shot preset machine and more like a small system you set up once and then play, letting a single sound adapt to whatever the arrangement needs next.
