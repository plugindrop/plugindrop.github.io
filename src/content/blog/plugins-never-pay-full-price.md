## Introduction

<!-- role:intro -->

Waves Platinum Bundle carries a steep list price, but tracked price history shows it consistently selling for a small fraction of that amount on every check logged since earlier in the year — the discounted price functions as the real price rather than a temporary markdown. This isn't an isolated case. Several well-known plugin makers show the same pattern in their pricing history: a high list price that almost nobody actually pays, paired with a recurring discount deep enough that paying full price is close to a mistake.

The pattern extends across a range of related products from these makers. Waves' Gold Bundle follows the same cycle as its Platinum sibling, dropping sharply during the brand's frequent flash sales. A cluster of well-regarded plugins — including console emulations, equalizers, compressors, and other processing tools from one major plugin maker — regularly fall to a single low flat price during a recurring storewide promotion covering any plugin in that catalog. Suites and individual tools from several other well-known plugin developers follow similar cycles, dropping during seasonal sales windows such as summer promotions, year-end release events, and major shopping holidays.

A list price only means something if people actually pay it. For the products covered here, tracked price history shows the official price appearing rarely, if at all, while a steep discount shows up far more often — making that recurring discount the price that actually matters when deciding when to buy.

## How to choose

<!-- role:choose -->

**Waves Platinum and Gold Bundles**

Waves advertises a high list price for the Platinum Bundle, but the amount it actually sells for rarely matches that figure. Across repeated checks spanning several months, the bundle consistently showed up at a steep discount, with only an occasional check landing anywhere close to the stated list price — and even then, not at the full amount.

Anyone considering the purchase should check the current live price rather than assume the listed figure is what they'll actually pay. The Gold Bundle follows the same pattern on a smaller scale: its stated price sits well above what flash sales bring it down to, and one particularly low price point has been recorded as its floor.

Waves runs promotional sales often enough that waiting for a lower price isn't really waiting for a rare event — it's closer to waiting a short while for the next one to come around.

**UAD 1176 Classic Limiter Collection**

Universal Audio periodically runs storewide sales that bring the price of the 1176 Classic Limiter Collection down substantially from its usual listed amount, with one especially low price point recorded during a past promotion. These storewide sales happen several times throughout the year rather than being limited to the Black Friday period, so anyone who assumes Black Friday is the only chance to get a lower price is working from an outdated assumption. A broader breakdown of how different brands handle seasonal discounting covers this pattern in more detail.

**Sonnox Oxford Inflator**

Oxford Inflator's standard price sits considerably higher than what it typically sells for during Sonnox's regular Summer Sale. On at least one occasion, a limited promotion covering every Sonnox plugin pushed the price even lower than that usual sale price, making it one of the deepest discounts seen among comparable plugins. When Sonnox does put a plugin on sale, the reduction tends to run deeper than what's typical elsewhere in this category.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the Waves Platinum Bundle actually worth its listed price?**

**Q: How often does the Plugin Alliance discount promotion happen?**

Even outside that special event, its regular seasonal sale price is already the deepest recurring discount among the products tracked here.
