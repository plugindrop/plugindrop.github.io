A: A multiband compressor splits audio into bands and applies compression to each independently, typically with fixed crossover points. A dynamic EQ applies gain reduction at specific frequencies based on signal level, using EQ-style bands. In 2026, many plugins — including FabFilter Pro-MB — operate as both, which has largely blurred the practical distinction for most mixing and mastering tasks.

## FAQ

<!-- role:faq -->

Q: What is the difference between a multiband compressor and a dynamic EQ?
A: A multiband compressor divides audio into separate frequency bands and compresses each one on its own, usually with fixed crossover points. A dynamic EQ instead reduces gain at particular frequencies based on how loud the signal gets, using EQ-style bands rather than fixed splits. Many current plugins, including FabFilter Pro-MB, can work as either one in the same tool, since each of its processing bands can be set up as a compressor, expander, or dynamic EQ node. That flexibility has made the line between the two types of tool much less clear for most mixing and mastering work.

Q: Should a multiband compressor be used on every mix bus?
A: No. It is meant to fix or shape specific problems rather than serve as a default insert on everything. It works well for things like a buildup of low-mid frequencies on a busy mix, harsh high-frequency energy on a vocal bus, or balance issues across frequency bands on a stereo master. Using it constantly on every bus tends to create an unnatural sound, where the frequency balance keeps shifting in a way that becomes noticeable and distracting.

Q: Can a multiband compressor be used on vocals?
A: Yes, though it should be applied carefully. It is particularly useful for controlling sibilance in the high end and the buildup caused by proximity effect in the low end. A dedicated de-esser is usually better suited to handling sibilance alone, but a multiband compressor offers wider, frequency-by-frequency control when a vocal needs more involved treatment across several ranges at once.

Q: Is FabFilter Pro-MB worth its cost?
A: For professional mixing and mastering work, yes. It offers up to six processing bands that can be placed anywhere in the spectrum or snapped together into a standard crossover setup, along with a minimum phase mode, linear-phase oversampling up to four times, and program- and frequency-dependent attack and release behavior. Because each band can act as a compressor, expander, or dynamic EQ, it covers tasks that would otherwise need a separate plugin, and its visual display helps make detailed decisions faster. Band solo, mute, and bypass controls add further flexibility. For producers working with a tighter budget, free or lower-cost alternatives are reasonable substitutes, but Pro-MB remains one of the most complete multiband tools available.

Q: What is OTT and why does it show up so often?
A: OTT is a free plugin that applies strong upward and downward multiband compression at the same time, across three bands. Its name refers to a well-known preset that originated in a popular music production program and became widely used in dubstep and EDM production. The heavily compressed, dense texture it produces became a signature sound in electronic music, which is why it keeps turning up across tutorials, project templates, and mixing sessions in that genre.
