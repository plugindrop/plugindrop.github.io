## Metering & Analysis

Accurate metering is the prerequisite for every other mastering decision. Streaming platforms use loudness normalization at specific LUFS targets, and a master submitted outside those parameters will sound quieter or dynamically squashed relative to everything around it. None of the tools in this section cost anything, so there's no purchase timing to think about here — install all three today.

The tools below span from a two-control brickwall to a multi-stage professional chain — and this is also the category where the paid upgrade decision (FabFilter Pro-L 2, covered below) actually matters most, so pay close attention to how close Limiter 6 GE gets before you consider spending $199.

## How to Choose

- **If you're releasing to streaming platforms,** start with Youlean Loudness Meter 2 — it has platform-specific LUFS presets built in and is the community-standard tool for Spotify and Apple Music compliance.
- **If you want a complete limiter chain in a single plugin,** Limiter 6 GE is the answer — it handles HF limiting, compression, clipping, brickwall, and True Peak in one interface and competes with tools costing $200.
- **If you want a no-setup brickwall limiter,** LoudMax has two controls and works — just understand it does not implement True Peak limiting.
- **If your master needs tonal correction,** use TDR Nova for dynamic, content-aware adjustments (like taming a resonant low-mid buildup), and TDR VOS SlickEQ for broad shelf corrections with a touch of analog character.
- **If you're ready for a paid upgrade,** FabFilter Pro-L 2 is the tool most cited by professional mastering engineers — but only invest in it after you have maximized what Limiter 6 GE can do for your specific workflow, and ideally only when it's on sale rather than at the $199 list price.
- **If you master frequently for clients,** iZotope Ozone 12's guided workflow can save real time per project, which is the actual justification for its price — not a capability gap in the free chain.

## FAQ
**Q: What LUFS target should I aim for in 2026?**
A: Spotify and YouTube both normalize to -14 LUFS Integrated; Apple Music normalizes to -16 LUFS; Tidal targets -14 LUFS. The practical standard is to master to -14 LUFS Integrated with a True Peak ceiling of -1 dBTP — this covers the widest range of platforms without requiring separate masters for each service. Youlean Loudness Meter 2 includes presets for each major platform.

**Q: Can free mastering plugins produce commercially competitive results?**
A: Community blind tests documented on Gearspace and KVR consistently show that Limiter 6 GE is indistinguishable from Pro-L 2 at moderate gain reduction, and TDR Nova holds up against paid dynamic EQ tools in direct comparisons. For most genres and most delivery destinations, the answer is yes — the ceiling is producer skill, not plugin cost, and that should factor directly into whether a $199 limiter purchase is actually the right next investment.

**Q: What is the correct plugin order for a free mastering chain?**
A: A standard signal path using these tools: SPAN (reference, always on) → TDR Nova (corrective EQ) → TDR Kotelnikov (gentle compression) → TDR VOS SlickEQ (tonal shaping) → Limiter 6 GE (brickwall and True Peak) → Youlean Loudness Meter 2 (final LUFS check). This order is not a rigid rule, but it reflects the processing hierarchy most mastering engineers follow.

**Q: Does LoudMax support True Peak limiting?**
A: No. LoudMax is a sample-peak brickwall limiter, not a True Peak limiter. Sample-peak limiting can allow inter-sample peaks above 0 dBFS to pass through, which streaming encoders can then clip during format conversion. For streaming delivery, Limiter 6 GE's True Peak stage is the more reliable choice. LoudMax is appropriate for offline rendering contexts or when inter-sample compliance is handled separately.

**Q: Is Limiter 6 GE genuinely free with no restrictions?**
A: Yes. Limiter 6 GE (the "GE" denotes the free edition) is fully functional with no time limit, no watermarking, and no feature gating compared to TBProAudio's paid Limiter 6 versions — the paid tiers add extra metering and workflow conveniences, not core limiting capability. This is a large part of why it's the most-cited proof point that a competitive mastering chain doesn't require a purchase in 2026.

**Q: Should I buy FabFilter Pro-L 2 or iZotope Ozone 12 first?**
A: It depends on what's actually missing from your workflow. If your bottleneck is limiting quality specifically — needing algorithm flexibility across different masters — Pro-L 2 is the more targeted purchase. If your bottleneck is workflow speed and you want a guided starting point across EQ, dynamics, and loudness in one pass, Ozone is the better fit, and its lower entry price (from $49 for Elements) makes it the cheaper way to test whether a paid mastering tool changes your results at all before committing to Pro-L 2's $199.
