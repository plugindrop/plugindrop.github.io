## SL 4000 B Circuit Modelling for a DAW Channel Strip

<!-- role:product -->

Released in 1976, the SL 4000 B console is the hardware lineage SSL models in the SSL 4K B channel strip. The plug-in is built to bring that B-series console design into a DAW, with detailed circuit modelling down to component level. SSL lists a Jensen JE-115K-E Transformer Mic Pre, dbx “black can” 202 VCA fader, the 4000 B EQ, and the 4000 B Compressor among the modelled elements.

For a producer new to the plug-in, a channel strip places several console-style processing sections in one plug-in. Here, those supported sections include the EQ and compressor. SSL says the 4000 B EQ preceded the Brown Knob EQ found on the 4000 E and describes it as “very musical sounding.” The compressor differs in its design: SSL says it is based on the Bus Compressor design, with peak detection, a Feedback VCA topology in the sidechain, and a unique De-Esser mode.

The supplied demo is titled *SSL 4K B: The “Rare” Analog Sound That Started It All*. Its title frames the product around the early SSL console lineage rather than a modern clean-channel-strip approach.

## Tone, Punch, and a Non-Linear Analogue Character

<!-- role:sound -->

SSL describes the 4K B as an authentic emulation “full of tone, punch and rich non-linear analogue character.” Those are the maker’s sound descriptors, tied directly to its modelling of the rare SL 4000 B. The historical console was installed in Studio 2, known as the Stone Room, at London’s Townhouse Studios; SSL also names recordings including Phil Collins’ *In the Air Tonight*, The Buggles’ *Video Killed the Radio Star*, and Peter Gabriel’s *Intruder* in its background for the console.

The maker positions this character against its own 9K-inspired Channel Strip 2. SSL calls the 4K B a companion for that cleaner product, so the distinction supplied by SSL is clear: 4K B is associated with tone, punch, and rich non-linear analogue character, while Channel Strip 2 is described as cleaner. That is a maker-stated positioning comparison, not a claim about an independently heard result.

## SSL 4K B Compared with CLA-76 and API 2500

<!-- role:peers -->

Peak detection and a Feedback VCA topology in the sidechain set the SSL 4K B compressor’s stated design apart from the peer facts available here. Waves describes CLA-76’s attack and release times as tracking drum dynamics, while its exclusive ALL ratio mode delivers an explosive compression sound. Those claims identify CLA-76 through its attack/release behaviour and ALL ratio mode; SSL instead identifies 4K B through B-series channel-strip modelling, peak detection, and its sidechain topology.

Waves API 2500 is described as an official emulation of the famed API bus compressor, modelled in close collaboration with API. SSL 4K B, by contrast, is presented as a full channel strip based on the SL 4000 B console, with an EQ and compressor among its modelled sections. The comparison is therefore one of stated product scope and lineage: the supplied API 2500 information centres on an API bus-compressor emulation, whereas SSL’s brief centres on a B-series console channel strip and a compressor based on the Bus Compressor design.

Neither comparison requires assuming a preferred sound. The available facts support different design descriptions: CLA-76 has an ALL ratio mode; API 2500 is an API bus-compressor emulation; and SSL 4K B combines SL 4000 B console modelling with its stated EQ, compressor, and De-Esser mode.
