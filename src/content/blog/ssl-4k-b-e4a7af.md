## What SSL 4K B does

SSL 4K B is a channel strip plug-in from SSL. In plain terms, it puts a set of console-inspired processing tools inside a DAW, based on SSL’s model of the rare SL 4000 B desk. The maker positions it as an authentic emulation of that console rather than a generic channel-strip design.

Its circuit modelling is described as reaching component level. The listed modelled elements include a Jensen JE-115K-E Transformer Mic Pre, a dbx “black can” 202 VCA fader, the 4000 B EQ, and the 4000 B Compressor. SSL says the B-series EQ preceded the Brown Knob EQ found on the 4000 E, and calls the 4000 B EQ “very musical.”

The compressor is a particularly specific part of the design. According to SSL, it is based on the Bus Compressor design, with peak detection and a feedback VCA topology in the sidechain. SSL also lists a unique De-Esser mode. For producers working in supported plug-in hosts, SSL 4K B is available as VST, VST3, AU, and AAX on compatible macOS systems; Windows versions are supplied in VST, VST3, and AAX.

The plug-in also works with SSL’s 360° Plug-in Mixer ecosystem. SSL lists first-class UC1 and UF8 integration through SSL 360° and the Plug-in Mixer, alongside Apple Silicon M1/ARM-native support.

## How SSL describes the sound

SSL describes 4K B as an authentic emulation “full of tone, punch and rich non-linear analogue character.” Those are the maker’s words, tied specifically to its model of the SL 4000 B console.

The SL 4000 B was released in 1976 and was installed in Studio 2, known as the Stone Room, at London’s Townhouse Studios. SSL says that console was used on records including Phil Collins’ *In the Air Tonight*, The Buggles’ *Video Killed the Radio Star*, and Peter Gabriel’s *Intruder*.

SSL frames the 4K B as a companion to the cleaner, 9K-inspired Channel Strip 2. That contrast is useful for understanding the stated intent: the 4K B is the B-series model with the maker’s claimed tone, punch, and rich non-linear analogue character, while SSL describes Channel Strip 2 as cleaner.

## SSL 4K B next to its peers

Waves CLA-76 is described as having attack and release times that track drum dynamics, while its exclusive ALL ratio mode delivers an “explosive compression sound.” SSL 4K B, by contrast, is presented as a channel strip based on the SL 4000 B console, with detailed circuit modelling that includes its EQ, a transformer mic pre, a VCA fader, and a compressor design.

Waves API 2500 is an official emulation of the famed API bus compressor, modelled in close collaboration with API. SSL 4K B has a different stated basis: SSL’s rare SL 4000 B console. Its compressor is based on the Bus Compressor design and uses peak detection with a feedback VCA topology in the sidechain; SSL also lists a De-Esser mode.

In other words, these listings identify different source designs and scopes. CLA-76 is presented around compression behavior and ALL ratio mode. API 2500 is presented as an API bus-compressor emulation. SSL 4K B is presented as an SL 4000 B-based channel strip with the maker’s component-level modelling claims.
