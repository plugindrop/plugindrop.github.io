## How to choose

<!-- role:choose -->

Choosing where to start depends on which gap matters most in your current setup.

For a single purchase this year, FabFilter Pro-Q 4 stands out. It is an EQ plug-in with up to 24 bands, each capable of dynamic EQ, along with a Spectral dynamics mode for treating problem frequencies precisely and efficiently. It runs across zero-latency, Natural Phase, and linear phase modes with less distortion and cleaner sound, and supports mid/side work for both mixing and mastering. Because it covers ground many native toolsets leave unaddressed, it tends to stay in use for years.

If reverb is the missing piece, Valhalla VintageVerb is worth considering as an algorithmic reverb option, and there is little reason to look further until a specific need arises that it cannot cover.

For a synthesis stack, the right choice depends on workflow: Serum suits electronic, pop, and bass-driven styles built around sound design, while Arturia Pigments 7 suits producers who want several synthesis types, including granular, additive, and wavetable, in one instrument.

Producers mixing and mastering their own releases without formal training may find iZotope Neutron 4 paired with Ozone 12 Elements practical. The guided starting points in both tools don't replace trained listening, but they ease the difficulty of simply getting started.

For creative effects without a big upfront commitment, Valhalla Supermassive and Kilohearts Snap Heap are reasonable first steps. Snap Heap is a modular host that lets you build up to seven serial or parallel effects chains and modulate every parameter through its own modulation system. Soundtoys 5 becomes worth considering once specific needs, such as tape-style delay or saturation, outgrow what free tools can meet.

## FAQ

<!-- role:faq -->

**Q: Are third-party VST plugins fully compatible with Studio One?**
A: Studio One supports VST3, VST2, and AU formats, with AU available only on macOS. Every plugin in this guide works with at least VST3, and most work with every available format.

**Q: Does FabFilter Pro-Q 4 actually outperform Studio One's built-in ProEQ3?**
A: For standard parametric EQ tasks, ProEQ3 performs well. The difference shows up with dynamic EQ, which lets any band apply frequency-specific compression, and with linear phase mode for mix-bus or mastering work. These are features built into Pro-Q 4 that have no native equivalent in current versions of Studio One.

**Q: What's the best free plugin to start with in Studio One?**
A: A good free plugin should give useful results right away with no extra setup. The practical test is whether it helps within the first few minutes of use, or whether it needs a tutorial before it becomes worth using.

**Q: Is the Soundtoys bundle worth it for a bedroom producer?**
A: Whether it's worth the full price depends on genre. For many workflows, the tape delay plugin alone can justify buying the set. The bundle goes on sale periodically at a meaningful discount, and buying at that lower price changes the per-plugin value considerably.

**Q: Should I buy iZotope Neutron 4 or FabFilter Pro-Q 4 first?**
A: These two plugins serve different roles. Pro-Q 4 rewards active mixing knowledge and keeps paying off as skill grows. Neutron 4's main value is its automatic starting-point feature, which helps most when a blank mix feels overwhelming. If you already understand EQ and want more precision, start with Pro-Q 4. If mixing feels like guesswork and you want an analyzable starting point, start with Neutron 4 instead.
