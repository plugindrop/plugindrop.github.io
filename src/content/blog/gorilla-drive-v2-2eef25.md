## What you actually get

Gorilla Drive V2 is a drum bus compressor plugin, and the vendor lists several updates over the original version. The new Transient Shaper puts snap and body balance on a single control, which speeds up getting a punchy drum bus without stacking extra plugins.

- The vendor lists independent Lows and Highs drive stages, letting you push saturation into the low end for weight without smearing the top end of the kit.
- According to the product listing, Ape Mode adds extra distortion and compression in one switch, useful for a quick "crush" setting on rooms or parallel buses.
- Battery emulation is listed as a way to drain the modeled voltage for a worn, unstable, more vintage-leaning grit.
- Smart Gain and Link Gain are listed for level matching, so A/B comparisons against the dry signal stay fair instead of being skewed by loudness.
- The listing also includes A/B compare, a short-term LUFS readout, and a new oversampling algorithm, which together are aimed at keeping gain-staging decisions accurate while mixing.
- The interface itself is listed as completely redesigned for V2, with a new preset system for recalling settings across sessions.

## Where it fits in a session

A drum bus compressor like this sits on the drum group bus, after individual track processing but before the compressed signal hits the main mix bus. That makes it a glue stage: taming transients across kick, snare, and overheads together rather than per track.

The Lows and Highs drive stages also make it workable as a parallel saturation return, blended under a cleaner drum bus for added weight. Ape Mode and the battery emulation point toward heavier, more aggressive drum treatments, which fits genres built around crushed or lo-fi drum character.
