Each filter gets its own drive, mix, feedback, dynamics, tone and level controls, plus solo and mute, so you can shape and audition each one on its own before combining them. ## FabFilter Simplon's Two-Filter Core
<!-- role:intro -->
<!-- pick:FabFilter Simplon -->

FabFilter Simplon takes the filtering engine from Volcano 2 and strips it down to something more direct. Two independent multimode filters sit at its core, and you can run them in serial or parallel, giving you two different ways to combine their character within a single patch.

Underneath that, Simplon offers Multiband processing (up to 6 bands), so a sound can pass through more than one filter stage at once if needed. Control happens per band: Per-band drive, mix, feedback, dynamics, tone and level controls let you dial in each stage on its own terms rather than applying one setting across the whole plugin. Once a band is set the way you want it, Per-band solo and mute options make it simple to isolate or silence specific parts while you keep adjusting the rest.

Rounding things out, Simplon includes an Optional HQ mode (8x oversampling), and Mid/side processing is available alongside it for working across the stereo image. An Interactive multiband display ties it all together, giving you a visual read of what each band is doing as you shape a patch.

## Inside the Controls
<!-- role:character -->

Simplon starts with Multiband processing (up to 6 bands), splitting a signal into as many as six separate bands you can treat on their own. Once those bands are set, the Per-band solo and mute options let you listen to or silence any single one, so you can check what each part is contributing before settling on a full mix. Mid/side processing adds a further option, letting you work on the center of a stereo signal apart from the sides. For situations that call for extra precision, Optional HQ mode (8x oversampling) is available as a switchable setting. The Interactive multiband display ties all of this together, showing every band on one screen so you can see and adjust them as you work.

## A Different Way to Split a Mix
<!-- role:workflow -->

Producers often reach for Simplon when they want to treat different parts of a sound separately instead of processing the whole signal at once. Multiband processing (up to 6 bands) lets you isolate a low end, a body, and a top end of a track and work on each piece on its own terms. You might want to add some grit to just the top of a vocal while leaving the low end untouched, or soften a cymbal wash without touching the kick underneath it. Per-band solo and mute options make it easy to check what each band is doing before you commit to a decision. On a stereo bus, mid/side processing lets you treat the center and the sides differently, which is useful when you want width without disturbing the mono core of a mix.
