## The Four Jobs of Drum Compression

Most producers learn compression backwards — they start with plugins instead of purposes. Before touching a compressor, know which of these four jobs you're trying to do:

1. **Individual compression** — controls dynamic variation between soft and hard hits, adds color
2. **Bus compression** — glues the whole kit, adds consistent energy and punch
3. **Parallel compression** — adds density and sustain while preserving the attack of the dry signal
4. **Transient shaping** — adjusts attack and sustain directly without touching dynamics

Each requires different settings and, arguably, different tools. Here's how to execute each one.

---

## Individual Drum Compression: Kick, Snare, and More

The goal on individual channels isn't heavy compression — it's consistency and character. You want hits to feel even without losing the energy of hard strikes.

### Kick Drum Settings

The kick has a sharp transient followed by a body tone. Your compressor needs to let that transient through, then grab the body before it fades too quickly.

**FabFilter Pro-C 2 settings for kick:**
- Attack: 10–30ms (let the transient through)
- Release: 80–120ms (Auto-release works well here)
- Ratio: 3:1 to 4:1
- Gain Reduction: 3–6dB
- Style: **Clean** or **Opto** depending on whether you want transparent or colored

**Klanghelm DC8C settings for kick (free alternative):**
- Set to **Type A** for a punchy, forward sound
- Attack: medium-slow, Release: medium
- Aim for 3–5dB of GR on the hardest hits

### Snare Drum Settings

Snare compression is where character matters most. The snare's crack lives in the transient; the body and room decay follow. Attack time determines how much of the crack survives.

**FabFilter Pro-C 2 settings for snare:**
- Attack: 5–15ms
- Release: 50–80ms
- Ratio: 4:1 to 6:1
- Style: **Punch** mode adds aggression — ideal for trap and hip-hop
- Gain Reduction: 4–8dB

For vintage snare character (rock, soul, boom-bap), the free **Klanghelm DC8C** in **Type B** mode beats most paid plugins at twice the price.

### Hi-Hats and Overheads

Overheads benefit from gentle, slow compression — fast settings make cymbals pump awkwardly.

- Attack: 50–80ms
- Release: 200–300ms
- Ratio: 2:1 to 3:1
- Gain Reduction: 2–4dB max

On overheads, **FabFilter Pro-C 2's Mastering mode** with a 2:1 ratio is clean enough that it sounds like a level adjustment, not compression.

## Bus Compression: Glue and Punch

Drum bus compression is where the kit starts sounding like a single instrument instead of separate channels. This is the most misunderstood application — producers often over-compress here, killing the transients that make drums feel live.

### The Classic SSL Sound

The **Waves SSL G-Master Buss Compressor** ($29) is the industry-standard starting point. It models the bus compressor from the SSL 4000G console, which has been on more hit records than any other piece of outboard gear.

**SSL G-Master Buss settings for drums:**
- Attack: **10ms** (the classic drum bus choice — lets the initial transient punch through)
- Release: **Auto** (tracks the program material)
- Ratio: **4:1**
- Threshold: Pull down until you see 2–4dB of GR on peaks
- Make-up gain: Match your output level

At $29, buying this plugin at full price is completely reasonable. It's been on perpetual sale for years — the "regular" price is essentially the sale price.

### When You Need More Character

For heavier compression with more vintage VCA color, look at **Cytomic The Glue** (~$49). It models the same SSL circuit with additional tone-shaping options. If you're doing rock or metal and want the bus compressor to be an audible part of the sound, The Glue delivers it.

## Parallel Compression: Density Without Killing Transients

Parallel compression (New York compression) keeps the uncompressed signal in the mix alongside a heavily compressed version. You get sustain and density without losing the crack of the transient.

### Setup in Any DAW

1. Send your drum bus to an aux channel at 100% wet
2. On the aux: ratio 8:1 to 10:1, attack 1–5ms, release 100–150ms, threshold set for 10–15dB of GR
3. Blend the aux into your mix at roughly 20–40% of the dry level

**Best plugin for parallel:** FabFilter Pro-C 2 in **Punch** or **Classic** style. The **Mix** knob on the Pro-C 2 lets you do this internally without a separate aux channel — dial in heavy settings, then pull the Mix knob back to 30–50% for built-in parallel compression.

---

## Transient Shaping: When Compression Isn't Enough

Sometimes you need to change the shape of a drum hit — add or remove attack, extend or shorten the sustain — without changing its dynamic behavior. Transient shapers do this job better than compressors.

**Recommended: Waves Smack Attack** (~$29) or **Flux BitterSweet v3** (free)

- Increase attack on a snare that sounds too soft in the mix
- Cut sustain on a boomy kick that's cluttering the low end
- Add sustain on a dry, dead snare to give it more room feel

These tools are especially useful when compression is already applied and you want to fine-tune the envelope rather than add another layer of dynamic processing.
