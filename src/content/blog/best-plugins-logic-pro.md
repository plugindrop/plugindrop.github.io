## FabFilter Pro-Q 4

<!-- role:pick -->

<!-- pick:FabFilter Pro-Q 4 -->

FabFilter Pro-Q 4 is a parametric EQ plug-in built to go beyond basic channel EQs, offering up to 24 bands along with per-band dynamic EQ processing. A full-spectrum real-time analyzer lets engineers compare levels between channels, and mid/side processing is included as standard, with no features locked behind higher pricing tiers. Its Natural Phase mode sits between the minimum phase and true linear phase approaches, giving users a middle-ground option for how the EQ affects timing and phase relationships in a mix.

The plug-in's standout addition is Spectral dynamics, which targets problem frequencies with more precision and efficiency than conventional EQ curves allow. Dynamic EQ bands come with adjustable Attack and Release controls, plus an optional free side-chain filter, so the EQ can react intelligently to specific parts of the signal rather than applying a static cut or boost. Overall dynamic EQ processing has been refined to introduce less distortion, resulting in cleaner sound whether working in zero-latency, Natural Phase, or linear phase modes.

This makes it well suited to both mixing and mastering work, including stereo, surround, and Dolby Atmos sessions. For mastering tasks that call for subtle, fine-grained moves, the display can be set to tighter 3 dB or 6 dB ranges, while mixing work that needs bigger, more dramatic adjustments can switch to wider 12 dB or 30 dB display ranges. In short, it's built for any mixing or mastering chain where precision and clear visual feedback on what's happening to the signal are top priorities.

u-he's Diva is an analog synth emulation that recreates, at the circuit level, the oscillator, filter, and envelope designs found in classic instruments such as the Minimoog, the Juno line, and Roland-style synth architectures, bringing that character together into one plugin. The developer's own documentation points to its Zero Delay Feedback filter modeling as central to how it reproduces that analog behavior, and the aim throughout is authentic, unrivaled analog sound rather than a stripped-down approximation. Compared with the previous version, this release runs noticeably lighter on the processor, cutting CPU load by as much as 40 percent, and it adds multithreading support so modern multi-core CPUs can handle more simultaneous voices without bogging down. The preset library has also grown, offering an even larger collection of organic-sounding starting points for sound design. The interface window can now be resized, with additional visual skins planned as a future addition.

Even with those efficiency gains, Diva still asks more of a computer than lighter, more modern instruments like Serum or Vital, so it remains comparatively demanding to run. It's built for producers who want genuinely analog-feeling tone for basses, pads, and leads and are willing to trade some convenience and processing headroom for that character, rather than those who prioritize the lowest possible CPU footprint. It runs on both Mac and Windows systems, with macOS support covering OS X 10.9 and newer on both Intel and Apple M1 chips, and Windows support covering Windows 7 and newer in both 32-bit and 64-bit versions, with at least 1GB of RAM recommended. It's distributed in VST, VST3, AU, and AAX plugin formats, making it compatible with the major production hosts on both platforms.

## Soundtoys Decapitator

<!-- role:pick -->

<!-- pick:Soundtoys Decapitator -->

Soundtoys Decapitator adds analog-style saturation to a mix or track by modeling five separate vintage circuit types: tape saturation, a transformer stage, a console input stage, a tube-style unit, and a limiter stage known for clipping hard when pushed. Each mode has its own color and behavior, letting the plugin go from subtle warmth to a thick, aggressive crunch depending on how it's driven. A dedicated "Punish" control pushes the saturation further for more extreme distortion, while a separate Tone control reshapes the high and low end of the processed signal.

The plugin is built to add harmonic density and texture to a sound without making the processing obviously audible, which makes it useful on drums, synths, submixes, or a full mix bus alike. It runs on Mac only and comes in AU and AAX formats, so it integrates directly into Logic Pro's plugin menus without any extra setup — the lack of Windows support isn't a drawback for anyone already working in Logic. In short, it's a flexible coloring tool for anyone who wants to make a track feel warmer, denser, or grittier using modeled analog gear rather than digital distortion.

**Q: Do VST plugins work in Logic Pro?**

A: Logic Pro natively uses the AU (Audio Units) format on macOS — not VST or VST3. Most professional third-party developers release AU versions for macOS alongside Windows VST builds, and all 15 plugins in this guide ship in AU format. If you encounter a macOS plugin that only supports VST3, a wrapper like Blue Cat's PatchWork can bridge the gap, but native AU is always preferable for stability.

**Q: Is FabFilter Pro-Q 4 worth the upgrade over Logic's Channel EQ?**

A: For most serious mixing work, yes. Logic's Channel EQ is a capable static equalizer, but Pro-Q 4 adds per-band dynamic EQ, a full-resolution spectrum analyzer with inter-channel comparison, Natural Phase mode, and native mid/side capability. The upgrade matters most for producers doing their own mix bus and mastering work.

**Q: Do these plugins run natively on Apple Silicon?**

A: As of 2026, every developer in this guide has released native Apple Silicon builds. FabFilter, Valhalla DSP, iZotope, Xfer Records, Soundtoys, u-he, oeksound, Klanghelm, and Tokyo Dawn Labs all confirmed Apple Silicon support in their release notes. Always verify the current system requirements on the developer's page before purchasing, as support timelines can vary between minor versions.

**Q: Is Vital actually as good as Serum?**

A: It depends on your workflow. Vital's spectral warping is a capability Serum doesn't offer, while Serum's preset library and third-party patch ecosystem are significantly larger. For pure synthesis capability, Vital is competitive at any price — but for working within a preset-driven production workflow, Serum's market depth is the real advantage.

## How to choose

<!-- role:choose -->

How to Choose

Anyone wanting the single most impactful plugin should start with FabFilter Pro-Q 4. Its dynamic EQ and spectrum analyzer change how a mix is heard, offering capability beyond Logic's stock Channel EQ, which has no real equivalent to those features.

Producers of electronic music who need a synthesizer first can download Vital at no cost to confirm that wavetable synthesis fits their workflow, then move to Serum once they want access to its much larger preset ecosystem.

Those mixing their own recorded audio can turn to Soothe2, which solves a specific resonance problem that no combination of stock EQ settings handles efficiently. It's worth adding only after confirming that resonance is actually the bottleneck in a given mix.

For anyone working with a tight budget, a free stack covers most core needs: TDR Nova handles dynamic EQ, MJUC jr. provides vintage bus compression, OTT delivers multiband compression, Vital supplies a capable wavetable synth, and Fresh Air works as a mix enhancer — all available without spending anything.

Among the lower-cost paid upgrades, Valhalla VintageVerb is a standout choice. Logic already covers convolution reverb well on its own, but algorithmic reverb of this quality is a genuine gap in the stock plugin library, and VintageVerb fills it.
