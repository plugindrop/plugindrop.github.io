## How to Choose

- **If you need ambient, lo-fi, or cinematic piano**, start with LABS Soft Piano. Its felt character is why it dominates community recommendations for those genres, and nothing free touches it there.
- **If you need a realistic acoustic grand for jazz, pop, or classical work**, Piano One or Salamander Grand Piano are the community's consensus picks for sample accuracy at zero cost.
- **If you need upright piano sounds**, Keyzone Classic covers the type in one install; PianoBook delivers more character and variety for producers willing to browse.
- **If you need vintage electric piano tones**, MrRay73 Mark II handles warm Rhodes territory; Dexed handles bright DX7 FM territory. They serve distinct aesthetics and are not interchangeable.
- **If you are ready to upgrade**, Spitfire Felt Piano extends the LABS approach with more expressive depth, and Arturia Piano V3 is the community-validated path to a physically modeled grand that responds to how you play, not just how hard.

## The Top 3 Free Picks, In Detail

### 1. LABS Soft Piano — Best for Ambient and Cinematic Work
Spitfire's felt-piano sampling is the reason this instrument keeps showing up in "best free VST" lists years after release. It's a narrow, intimate sound rather than a full-range concert grand, which makes it correct for lo-fi beats, film-score sketches, and anything that needs a piano to sit *behind* a mix instead of in front of it. If that's not the tonal character you need, skip straight to Piano One below — LABS won't stretch to fill that role.

### 2. Piano One — Best for a Clean, Realistic Grand
Sound Magic's multi-velocity Yamaha-based sampling is the closest thing free software gets to a "does everything" acoustic grand. It's the pick for producers who need a piano that doesn't announce itself sonically — pop ballads, jazz comping, singer-songwriter demos. Pair it with Salamander Grand Piano (via a free SFZ player like Sforzando) if you want an even more detailed alternative to A/B against.

### 3. MrRay73 Mark II — Best Free Electric Piano
GSi's physically modeled Rhodes Mark II is warmer and more dynamically responsive than most sample-based electric pianos at any price point below $50. It's the free plugin most likely to survive into a final mix untouched. Dexed, running the full DX7 preset library through open-source FM synthesis, covers the brighter, glassier '80s-style electric piano tones that MrRay73 doesn't attempt.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Spitfire LABS Soft Piano actually free — no trial period, no watermark?**
A: Yes. Spitfire Audio offers LABS instruments as permanently free downloads. You create a free account, install the LABS plugin, and claim the instrument. There is no expiry, no watermark on renders, and no paid tier required to unlock its features.

**Q: What format do free piano VSTs come in, and will they work in my DAW?**
A: Most plugins here are distributed as VST (Windows) or VST/AU (macOS). All major DAWs — Ableton Live, FL Studio, Reaper, Logic Pro, Cubase — support these formats. The exception is Salamander Grand Piano, which is an SFZ sample library requiring a separate free SFZ player like Sforzando by Plogue before it behaves like a standard instrument.

**Q: What is the best free electric piano VST in 2026?**
A: MrRay73 Mark II by GSi is the community's top pick for warm, physical-modeled Rhodes tones. Dexed is the recommendation for DX7-style FM electric piano — brighter, glassier, and more suited to 1980s-influenced production. They cover different sonic territory and are worth installing both.

**Q: Do free piano VSTs require a powerful computer?**
A: Synthesis-based options — 4Front Piano, MDA EPiano, Dexed — have minimal CPU and RAM requirements. Sample-based options like Piano One and Salamander Grand Piano benefit from 2–4 GB of available RAM and an SSD for fast loading. LABS Soft Piano streams efficiently through the LABS platform but still benefits from faster storage.

**Q: Can I use these free plugins in commercial releases?**
A: All plugins listed here are documented for commercial use. Spitfire LABS instruments are explicitly licensed for commercial productions. Sound Magic, Bitsonic, DSK Music, and the open-source MDA and Dexed projects permit commercial use under their respective licenses. Verify the current license for any plugin you download, as terms can update.

**Q: Is it ever worth paying for a piano VST if free options exist?**
A: Yes, specifically when a session needs expressive nuance or acoustic resonance that sampled free instruments can't reproduce — exposed piano ballads and solo piano pieces are the most common cases. For everything else (layered arrangements, lo-fi, background parts), the free tier is usually indistinguishable in the final mix.
