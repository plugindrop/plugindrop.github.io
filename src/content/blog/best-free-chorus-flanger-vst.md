**Q: What's the actual difference between a chorus and a flanger?**

A: Both mix a delayed copy of the signal back with the original, with modulation applied to the delay time. Chorus uses longer delay times — typically 20–30ms — to create a rich, doubled or multi-voice effect. Flanging uses much shorter delay times — often 1–10ms — which produces the characteristic jet-sweep or comb-filtering sound. The controls look similar on the surface, but the audible results are distinct enough that they serve different purposes in a mix.

Q: What is the difference between a chorus effect and a flanger effect?
A: Both effects mix a delayed copy of a signal back with the original sound, with the delay time modulated over time. Chorus uses longer delay times, generally in the range of twenty to thirty milliseconds, to create a thickened, multi-voice sound. Flanging uses much shorter delay times, often only one to ten milliseconds, which creates the distinctive sweeping or comb-filtering sound. The controls on each type of plugin look similar, but the resulting sound is different enough that the two effects are used for different purposes in a mix.
