## Why Valhalla DSP Has No Sales

Sean Costello, the DSP engineer behind Valhalla DSP, has said publicly and repeatedly that his plugins will not go on sale — not on Black Friday, not during a "flash sale," not ever. Every Valhalla plugin (VintageVerb, Room, Plate, Delay, ÜberMod, Freq Echo, and Space Modulator) is priced at a flat **$50**, and that price has held since launch. There's no "regular price" being marked down to make $50 feel special — $50 simply *is* the price, permanently.

The reasoning is philosophical, not marketing: Costello has argued that artificial scarcity and countdown-timer sales pressure people into impulse purchases they don't need, and that a fair, stable price is more respectful of both the product and the customer. So if you're holding out for a Valhalla Black Friday deal, a bundle discount, or a coupon code, stop — it isn't coming.

## Is $50 Actually a Good Price?

Yes — and this is the part that gets lost when people focus only on "no sales." Compare Valhalla's $50 flat rate to what competing reverbs cost even *during* a sale:

- FabFilter Pro-R2 typically runs $179–$299, with sales usually landing around $200. See [When Does FabFilter Pro-R Go On Sale?](/blog/when-does-fabfilter-pro-r-go-on-sale) for typical discount timing.
- Eventide Blackhole and other Eventide reverbs list around $99–$199 depending on the promo.
- Liquidsonics Seventh Heaven and Cinematic Rooms sit in the $150–$300 range.

At $50, VintageVerb or Room isn't a discount version of a premium plugin — it's a full-featured, professionally regarded reverb that undercuts the sale price of most competitors while never itself being marked down. Producers who've compared it against pricier options consistently rank it as one of the best value-per-dollar reverbs on the market, sale or no sale.

## Try Before You Buy: Valhalla Supermassive

Before spending anything, install **Valhalla Supermassive** — it's completely free, no time limit, no watermark, and covers ambient reverb, granular delay, and shimmer-style textures. It's built on the same DSP quality as the paid plugins, just with a narrower, effects-focused feature set (it's not meant to replace VintageVerb as a mix-bus reverb).

If Supermassive makes you want more control — real-time-adjustable early reflections, a wider range of algorithms, mix-bus-ready room and plate modeling — that's your signal to spend the $50 on VintageVerb, Room, or Plate. You'll already know exactly what Valhalla's sound and workflow feel like before paying anything.

## When to Buy

- **Buy VintageVerb, Room, or Plate now** if you're actively mixing or producing and need a dependable reverb today. There's no future discount to wait for, so delaying only costs you the plugin's use in the meantime.
- **Start with Supermassive (free)** if you're still building your template or unsure which Valhalla plugin fits your workflow. Upgrade to a paid plugin once you hit its limits.
- **Don't wait for Black Friday, Cyber Monday, or a coupon code** — Valhalla doesn't run them, and there is no historical precedent of the $50 price moving in either direction.
- **Budget $50 per plugin, not per bundle** — Valhalla doesn't sell suite bundles, so if you want VintageVerb *and* Delay, that's $100 total, at any time of year.
- **If price is the blocker, not timing**, use the free alternatives above rather than waiting — no future sale will make $50 more affordable than it already is.
